Five more images tracing the arc from Singapore's fall in 1942, through the occupation that produced the Sook Ching massacre, to the artefacts its mass graves gave up two decades later.

### The fall of Singapore, 15 February 1942

![Lieutenant General Yamashita Tomoyuki and Lieutenant General A.E. Percival discuss surrender terms, 15 February 1942](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/40/Yamashita_and_Percival_discuss_surrender_terms.jpg)

*Lieutenant General Yamashita Tomoyuki and Lieutenant General A.E. Percival discuss surrender terms at the Ford Motor Factory near Bukit Timah Road, 15 February 1942 — the day Singapore fell, and the start of the occupation that produced Sook Ching. (Photo: Imperial War Museums / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### Japanese troops enter the city

![Japanese troops marching through Singapore's city centre, February 1942](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/15/JapaneseMarchSgpCity.jpg)

*Victorious Japanese troops march through Singapore's city centre shortly after the British surrender, February 1942. (Photo: Imperial War Museums / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### Life under occupation, as "Syonan-to"

![A Shinto shrine in Shonan (Singapore), October 1942](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/80/Shinto_shrine_in_Shonan_%28Singapore%29_-_194210.jpg)

*A Shinto shrine built in occupied Singapore, by then renamed Syonan-to ("Light of the South"), photographed in October 1942. (Photo: Mainichi Newspapers Co.,* Showa History Vol. 10: Pacific War Breaks Out *, scanned by Wikimedia Commons user Abasaa, public domain)*

### The occupation ends, 12 September 1945

![Japanese Surrender at Singapore, 12 September 1945](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c8/Japanese_Surrender_at_Singapore%2C_12_September_1945_A30492.jpg)

*Admiral Lord Louis Mountbatten reads the terms of surrender to Japanese delegates at Singapore, 12 September 1945, formally ending three and a half years of occupation. (Photo: Royal Navy official photographer / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### What the mass graves gave up

![Personal items recovered from Sook Ching mass graves, on display at the National Museum of Singapore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a4/Items_found_in_mass_graves_due_to_the_Sook_Ching_massacre_of_1942_by_the_Japanese.jpg)

*Personal items recovered from the Sook Ching mass graves uncovered around Singapore from 1962 onward, now held at the National Museum of Singapore. (Photo: Wombatjpw / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*
