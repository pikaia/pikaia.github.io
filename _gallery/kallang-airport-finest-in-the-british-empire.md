A few more views of Kallang Airport — the pre-war civil aerodrome, the wartime field, and the terminal building that outlived it — from the 1930s to today.

### The airport from the air, 1945

![A 1945 aerial photograph of Kallang Airport showing the curved terminal building and control tower at lower left, the grass landing circle, and the Kallang Basin crowded with boats and stilt houses](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d4/Kallang_Airport_aerial_photo_1945.jpg)

*Kallang Airport photographed from the air on 26 September 1945, weeks after the Japanese surrender. The terminal and its round control tower stand at lower left, with the grass landing circle beyond and the Kallang Basin — the flying-boat landing area — full of small craft. Photograph by Lieutenant R. J. Buchanan; Australian War Memorial (119757), public domain.*

### The runway from an approaching aircraft, 1945

![An oblique aerial view of Kallang aerodrome in 1945, taken from an aircraft on approach, showing the concrete runway crossing the field with the sea beyond](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e4/Kallang_Airport_runway_1945.jpg)

*The Kallang aerodrome seen from an aircraft coming in to land, 9 September 1945. The concrete runway across the old grass field was laid by the Japanese military during the occupation. Photographer unidentified; Australian War Memorial (116979), public domain.*

### The field in its setting, 1945

![A 1945 aerial photograph looking across the town and harbour of Singapore, with the grass landing circle of Kallang Airport visible at the top edge](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3c/Kallang_Airport_and_Basin_area_1945.jpg)

*Part of the town and harbour, taken from an RAF aircraft shortly after the Japanese surrender in 1945. Kallang's grass landing circle is at the top of the frame — a reminder of how close to the centre of the city the airport was. Photographer unidentified; Australian War Memorial (SUK14720), public domain.*

### A KLM DC-3 at Singapore, 1939

![A gleaming bare-metal KLM Douglas DC-3 airliner named De Reiger parked on the apron at Singapore in 1939, being refuelled, with ground crew in white uniforms around it](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/KITLV_A782_-_De_Reiger_van_het_type_DC-3_van_de_Koninklijke_Luchtvaart_Maatschappij_onderweg_naar_Nederland_op_het_vliegveld_te_Singapore_02.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_A782_-_De_Reiger_van_het_type_DC-3_van_de_Koninklijke_Luchtvaart_Maatschappij_onderweg_naar_Nederland_op_het_vliegveld_te_Singapore_02.tif.jpg)

*A KLM Douglas DC-3, "De Reiger", being serviced on the apron at Singapore in 1939, on its way back to the Netherlands. The Dutch airlines were among Kallang's regular pre-war users. Leiden University Library (KITLV collection), [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).*

### An RAF Liberator on the apron, September 1945

![Three Royal Air Force airmen walking across the apron at Kallang in front of a Consolidated B-24 Liberator bomber, with the terminal building and round control tower behind](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/57/RAF_Liberator_aircraft_at_Kallang_Airport%2C_Singapore_-_194509.jpg)

*Royal Air Force personnel pass a Consolidated B-24 Liberator at Kallang in September 1945, with Frank Dorrington Ward's terminal and control tower behind. Photograph by Flight Officer Ashley, Royal Air Force; public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.*

### Abandoned Japanese fighters, September 1945

![A Royal Air Force officer standing on a step beside the dappled-camouflage nose of an abandoned Japanese Kawasaki Ki-45 twin-engined fighter at Kallang, with more of the aircraft in a line behind](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/40/Japanese_Kawasaki_Ki-45_Toryu_aircraft_at_Kallang_Airport%2C_Singapore_-_194509.jpg)

*A Royal Air Force officer examines a Kawasaki Ki-45 Toryu — one of a line of Japanese aircraft left behind at Kallang at the end of the war. Photograph by Flight Officer Ashley, Royal Air Force; public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.*

### The terminal as the People's Association headquarters, 2006

![The former Kallang Airport terminal building in 2006, photographed from the street at an angle, in use as the headquarters of the People's Association](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/af/Former_People%27s_Association_headquarters_at_old_Kallang_Airport%2C_Singapore.jpg)

*The building in 2006, during its long second life as the headquarters of the People's Association (1960–2009). Photo by Terence Ong, [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/), via Wikimedia Commons.*

### The empty terminal from the air, 2021

![A drone photograph of the former Kallang Airport terminal in 2021, empty, its streamline curves and green-glazed control tower set against the high-rise towers of Kallang Riverside](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Kafv210117.jpg/1280px-Kafv210117.jpg)

*The former terminal in 2021, empty, with the condominium towers of Kallang Riverside rising behind it. Photo by Daniel Taka, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), via Wikimedia Commons.*

### The Straits Settlements crest on the gate

![A close view of a cast metal heraldic emblem on the gate of the old Kallang Airport — a lion standing before a coconut palm, the charge of the Settlement of Singapore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Charge_of_the_Settlement_of_Singapore_on_the_gate_of_Kallang_Airport%2C_Singapore_-_20110501.jpg/960px-Charge_of_the_Settlement_of_Singapore_on_the_gate_of_Kallang_Airport%2C_Singapore_-_20110501.jpg)

*The "lion against a coconut tree" — the charge of the Settlement of Singapore from the arms of the Straits Settlements — on the airport's gate, restored in the 1994 conservation works. Photo by Jacklee, [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/), via Wikimedia Commons.*
