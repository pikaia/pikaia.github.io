More views of the Tiger Balm garden on Pasir Panjang Road — its gates and grounds, its pagoda and ponds, and the Ten Courts of Hell in closer detail.

### The entrance in 1960

![The entrance to Haw Par Villa in September 1960](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b5/TigerBalmGarden-HawParVilla-entrance-Singapore-196009.jpg)

*The entrance to the gardens in September 1960, in the family's era, when admission was free and the statues were Boon Haw's post-war rebuilding. (Peter Forster, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 1960)*

### The Chinese arch, 2014

![The Chinese arch at the entrance of Haw Par Villa in 2014](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0b/Chinese_arch_at_entrance_of_Haw_Par_Villa_%2814791657844%29.jpg)

*The entrance arch photographed in 2014, before the conservation works of 2020–21 restored the main arch. (Jnzl's Photos, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2014)*

### The hilly grounds

![Wide view over the hilly grounds of Haw Par Villa](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a2/Hilly_grounds_of_Haw_Par_Villa_%2814813779273%29.jpg)

*The terraced hillside the garden climbs, tier upon tier of painted figures among the trees. (Jnzl's Photos, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2014)*

### The pagoda

![The towering pagoda of Haw Par Villa](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ef/Towering_pagoda%2C_Haw_Par_Villa_%2814607177310%29.jpg)

*The pagoda was built fifteen storeys tall in 1937; Boon Haw later had the top storeys replaced with a statue of Sakyamuni, leaving four. (Jnzl's Photos, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2014)*

### The bridge to the pavilion

![The bridge connecting to the pavilion island on the pond](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5c/Bridge_connecting_to_pavilion_island%2C_Haw_Par_Villa_%2814770947476%29.jpg)

*The bridge to the pavilion island on one of the garden's ponds, in the Chinese garden manner the grounds were laid out in. (Jnzl's Photos, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2014)*

### The Sixth Court of Hell

![A body sawn in two in the Sixth Court of Hell](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/55/Body_sawn_into_two%2C_Sixth_Court_of_Hell_%E2%80%93_Yama_King_Piencheng%2C_Haw_Par_Villa_%2814813818183%29.jpg)

*In the Sixth Court, over which Yama King Piencheng presides, a body is sawn in two — the Courts' punishments were fitted, sin by sin, to the crime. (Jnzl's Photos, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2014)*

### Inside the Courts

![A Ten Courts of Hell tableau in close view](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/db/Chinese_Court_of_Hell.jpg)

*A court tableau in close view: judges, guards and the judged, modelled and painted by the garden's craftsmen and repainted across the decades since. (ScribblingGeek, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2019)*

### The monkey army

![The monkey army tableau at Haw Par Villa](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f6/Monkey_army%2C_Haw_Par_Villa_%2814791531714%29.jpg)

*The monkey army — the garden's folklore tableaux drew on Journey to the West and the Chinese story tradition the Swatow craftsmen knew by heart. (Jnzl's Photos, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2014)*

### The animal parade

![The animal parade: anthropomorphic animals as musicians](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ab/Animal_parade%2C_Haw_Par_Villa_%2814770865746%29.jpg)

*The animal parade, its musicians in human dress — the zoo the garden began with was soon replaced by animal statues, which were easier to keep. (Jnzl's Photos, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2014)*

### The brothers themselves

![Statues of Aw Boon Haw and Aw Boon Par at Haw Par Villa](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c4/Aw_Boon_Haw%2C_Aw_Boon_Par%2C_Haw_Par_Villa_%2814813863263%29.jpg)

*Aw Boon Haw and Aw Boon Par, commemorated in their own garden. When the family donated the statues to the nation in 1985, its condition was that the Haw Par name and the family memorials be retained. (Jnzl's Photos, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2014)*
