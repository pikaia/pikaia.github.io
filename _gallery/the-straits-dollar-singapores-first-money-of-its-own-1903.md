More images of the coins and notes of the Straits dollar, the money it replaced, and the waterfront of banks where the business of exchange was done.

### The 1903 dollar, another example

![Front and back of a silver Straits Settlements dollar of 1903 with the crowned head of Edward VII](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ef/Straits_settlements-1Dollar-1903.jpg)

*Another Straits Settlements dollar of 1903. (As6022014, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The design in close-up

![The reverse of a Straits Settlements one-dollar coin of 1904, an ornate interlaced design with Chinese and Jawi script and the words ONE DOLLAR](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/Straits_Settlements%2C_One_Dollar%2C_Edward_VII%2C_1904%2C_-_reverse.jpg/1280px-Straits_Settlements%2C_One_Dollar%2C_Edward_VII%2C_1904%2C_-_reverse.jpg)

*The reverse of a Straits dollar of 1904, with its value in English, Chinese and Jawi. (5snake5, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The gold sovereign

![A gold British sovereign of Edward VII showing the king's head in profile](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Edward_VII_sovereign_MET_DP100405.jpg/1280px-Edward_VII_sovereign_MET_DP100405.jpg)

*A gold sovereign of Edward VII, 1902. From 1906, $60 in Straits notes could be exchanged for seven of these. (George William de Saulles and Benedetto Pistrucci, Metropolitan Museum of Art, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The Mexican dollar it replaced

![A Mexican silver eight-reales coin of 1888, front and back, stamped with many small Chinese merchants' chop marks](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/1888_M%C3%A9xico_8_Reals_Trade_Coin_Silver.jpg/1280px-1888_M%C3%A9xico_8_Reals_Trade_Coin_Silver.jpg)

*A Mexican silver dollar of 1888, stamped with Chinese merchants' chop marks. Mexican dollars stopped being legal tender in the Straits in 1904. (Centpacrr, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A fifty-dollar note

![A Straits Settlements fifty-dollar banknote in purple and blue, with a tiger at the centre and Chinese and Jawi script](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/02/Straits_Setlements_-_1911_-_%2450_banknote.jpg)

*A fifty-dollar note of the Government of the Straits Settlements, printed by De La Rue. (De La Rue, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Governor Anderson

![Full-length photograph of Sir John Anderson in a white dress uniform with decorations, standing beside a table](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4a/Sir_john_anderson.gif)

*Sir John Anderson, Governor of the Straits Settlements from 1904 to 1911, under whom the dollar was fixed in 1906. (G. R. Lambert, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Commercial Square, 1904

![A street of tall Victorian commercial buildings on Commercial Square, Singapore, with a postcard caption reading Commercial Square, Singapore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/53/Collyer_Quay%2C_Singapore_1900s.jpg)

*Commercial Square and Collyer Quay on a postcard of about 1904, with the banks and merchant houses where exchange was traded. (Unknown photographer, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The Hongkong and Shanghai Bank

![The Hongkong and Shanghai Bank building in Singapore around 1900, a grand building with arched verandahs and towers](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/KITLV_-_50198_-_Lambert_%26_Co.%2C_G.R._-_Singapore_-_Building_of_the_Hong_Kong_and_Shanghai_Bank_in_Singapore_-_circa_1900.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_-_50198_-_Lambert_%26_Co.%2C_G.R._-_Singapore_-_Building_of_the_Hong_Kong_and_Shanghai_Bank_in_Singapore_-_circa_1900.tif.jpg)
