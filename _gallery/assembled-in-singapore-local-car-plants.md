Period photography of Singapore's car assembly lines barely exists, but here are a few more images around the story — the Bukit Timah Ford building then and now, the surrender it is remembered for, and the electric cars being built in Jurong today.

### A 1924 Ford Model T

![An unrestored 1924 Ford Model T Tourer with wooden-spoke wheels and an open bonnet, on display in a museum](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/1924_Ford_T_Tourer_%2814467176824%29.jpg/1280px-1924_Ford_T_Tourer_%2814467176824%29.jpg)

*Ford's first Singapore operation, in a rented shophouse off Enggor Street from 1926, did little more than fit wheels to imported Model Ts like this one and touch up the paint. (Photo: Sicnag / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/); this example is in a museum in the United States.)*

### The surrender, 15 February 1942

![Lieutenant-General Percival and three British officers, one carrying a large Union flag, walking along a road towards the Japanese lines to surrender Singapore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Surrender_Singapore.jpg/1280px-Surrender_Singapore.jpg)

*Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival's party carries the Union flag towards the Japanese lines on 15 February 1942. The surrender was signed inside the Ford assembly plant at Bukit Timah. (Official photograph, 1942; public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

### The Former Ford Factory today

![The white Art Deco frontage of the Former Ford Factory in 2025, with its stepped pilasters and tall narrow windows](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Former_Ford_Factory%2C_October_2025.jpg/1280px-Former_Ford_Factory%2C_October_2025.jpg)

*The Former Ford Factory on Upper Bukit Timah Road, October 2025. The Art Deco block was designed by Émile Brizay and opened in 1941. (Photo: GoAheadFan95 / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).)*

### Inside the museum

![The interior of the Former Ford Factory museum, with exhibition panels and a recreated room](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Old_Ford_Motor_Factory_-_www.joyofmuseums.com_-_internal.jpg/1280px-Old_Ford_Motor_Factory_-_www.joyofmuseums.com_-_internal.jpg)

*Inside the building, now the "Surviving the Japanese Occupation" gallery run by the National Archives of Singapore. (Photo: Joyofmuseums / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).)*

### "Peace"

![A bronze sculpture titled "Peace" by Chua Boon Kee outside the Old Ford Motor Factory](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f3/Peace_by_Chua_Boon_Kee%2C_Old_Ford_Motor_Factory%2C_Singapore_-_20070316.jpg)

*The sculpture "Peace" by Chua Boon Kee, outside the former factory. (Photo: Sengkang / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/).)*

### The monument plaque

![A national monument plaque mounted at the entrance to the Former Ford Factory](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Plaque%28former-ford-factory%29.jpg/1280px-Plaque%28former-ford-factory%29.jpg)

*The plaque at the entrance. The building was gazetted as a national monument on 15 February 2006, 64 years to the day after the surrender. (Photo: Ckwongb / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).)*

### Temasek Shophouse

![The restored Temasek Shophouse building at 28 Orchard Road, a cream-coloured 1928 shophouse block with arched windows](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Temasek_Shophouse%2C_Singapore_-_03.jpg/1280px-Temasek_Shophouse%2C_Singapore_-_03.jpg)

*The 1928 shophouse block at 28 Orchard Road, next door to the former Malayan Motors car showroom. It sold furniture and radios in its day; restored and reopened in 2019 as Temasek Shophouse, a philanthropy hub open to the public. (Photo: Sgconlaw / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).)*
