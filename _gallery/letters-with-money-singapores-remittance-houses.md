More images of the people, ports and streets at both ends of the remittance trade, from Singapore's Chinese quarter to the harbours of Amoy and Swatow and the remittance offices that still stand in southern China.

### Seah Eu Chin

![A portrait of Seah Eu Chin in old age, with a white moustache and beard, wearing a dark skullcap and jacket](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Seah_Eu_Chin.jpg/1280px-Seah_Eu_Chin.jpg)

*Seah Eu Chin, the Teochew merchant whose 1847 account of remittances by junk is the earliest description of the trade in Singapore. (From Song Ong Siang, One Hundred Years' History of the Chinese in Singapore, 1923, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Governor Sir William Jervois

![A seated photograph of Sir William Jervois in military dress uniform with a sash, orders and a sword, with a full moustache and side whiskers](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Portrait_of_Governor_Jervois%28GN02384%29.jpg/1280px-Portrait_of_Governor_Jervois%28GN02384%29.jpg)

*Sir William Jervois, Governor of the Straits Settlements from 1875 to 1877, whose Chinese Sub-Post Office set off the 1876 riot. Photographed around 1885, when he was Governor of South Australia. (State Government Photographer, State Library of South Australia, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Junks off Tanjong Katong, 1879

![A watercolour of Chinese junks with ribbed sails in a rainstorm off a sandy shore with a thatched shelter](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/John_Edmund_Taylor%2C_Chinese_Junks_in_a_Squall._Singapore._%281879%2C_Wellcome_V0037495%29.jpg/1280px-John_Edmund_Taylor%2C_Chinese_Junks_in_a_Squall._Singapore._%281879%2C_Wellcome_V0037495%29.jpg)

*Chinese junks in a squall off Tanjong Katong, painted in 1879. Before steamships, letters and money went home on the junks' seasonal voyages. (John Edmund Taylor, Wellcome Collection, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A junk at Tanjong Katong

![A two-masted Chinese junk at anchor on calm water, on a postcard captioned Chinese Junk carrying Firewood, Tanjong Katong, Singapore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6a/566._Chinese_Junk_caring_Firewood%2C_Tanjong_Katon%2C_Singapore%2C_KITLV_1404877.tiff/lossy-page1-1280px-566._Chinese_Junk_caring_Firewood%2C_Tanjong_Katon%2C_Singapore%2C_KITLV_1404877.tiff.jpg)

*A Chinese junk carrying firewood off Tanjong Katong, on a postcard of between 1920 and 1940. (Abdon, Penang, KITLV, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Amoy harbour, about 1869

![Amoy harbour around 1869, with a large balanced boulder on the shore in the foreground and ships at anchor across the water](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Amoy_Harbour_MET_DP165636.jpg/1280px-Amoy_Harbour_MET_DP165636.jpg)

*Amoy (Xiamen) harbour around 1869. Amoy was one of the two ports, with Swatow, through which Singapore's Chinese letters reached their villages. (John Thomson, Metropolitan Museum of Art, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Migrants from Shantou

![Chinese labourers carrying bundles walking down a gangway from a steamship onto a wooden wharf](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/db/COLLECTIE_TROPENMUSEUM_Chinese_koelies_uit_Shantou_verlaten_het_schip_%27s_Jacob_in_de_haven_van_Belawan_Sumatra_om_in_de_tabakcultuur_te_gaan_werken_TMnr_10001445.jpg)

*Contract labourers from Shantou (Swatow) coming ashore at Belawan in Sumatra to work on the tobacco plantations, between 1900 and 1915. Migrants like them across the region sent money home through the remittance houses, many of them by way of Singapore. (Unknown photographer, Tropenmuseum, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A Chinese street in Singapore, 1860

![A Singapore street in 1860 decorated with a tall ceremonial arch, flags and lanterns, with shophouses behind](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/KITLV_-_29169_-_Chinese_street_in_Singapore%2C_completely_decorated_on_the_occasion_of_the_visit_of_Prince_Albert_of_Saxe-Coburg_-_Gotha%2C_husband_of_Queen_Victoria_-_1860.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-thumbnail.tif.jpg)

*A Chinese street in Singapore decorated for a royal visit in 1860, in the years when the shuike couriers carried letters and money home. (Unknown photographer, KITLV, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Thian Hock Keng, about 1890

![The Thian Hock Keng temple in Singapore around 1890, with its curved, ornamented roofs and a broad forecourt](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/KITLV_-_103742_-_Lambert_%26_Co._-_Thian_Hock_Keng_Temple_in_Singapore_-_circa_1890.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_-_103742_-_Lambert_%26_Co._-_Thian_Hock_Keng_Temple_in_Singapore_-_circa_1890.tif.jpg)

*The Hokkien temple of Thian Hock Keng on Telok Ayer Street around 1890, at the heart of the district where many remittance houses did business. (G. R. Lambert and Co., KITLV, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A street vendor, about 1890

![A studio photograph of a Chinese street vendor in a wide hat standing beside his wares](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/10/KITLV_-_103776_-_Chinese_street_vendor_in_Singapore_-_circa_1890.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_-_103776_-_Chinese_street_vendor_in_Singapore_-_circa_1890.tif.jpg)

*A Chinese street vendor in Singapore around 1890, one of the many working people whose small savings went home through the remittance houses. (Unknown photographer, KITLV, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*
