More on the trade that ran the Singapore River, and what stands where it used to work.

### A surviving godown

![A 1950s-era warehouse (godown) with a corrugated roof, still standing on Moonstone Lane beside the Kallang River, fenced off](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Warehouse_at_Moonstone_Lane.jpg/1280px-Warehouse_at_Moonstone_Lane.jpg)

*A 1950s warehouse still standing at Moonstone Lane, on the Kallang River — one of the few riverside godowns of this era left in Singapore. (Photo: M. Adrian Jurhami, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

### River trade, in bronze

![A bronze sculpture of merchants and a bullock cart on the Singapore River bank, part of the People of the River series](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/62/The_River_Merchant_Sculpture._Singapore._%288069678494%29.jpg/1280px-The_River_Merchant_Sculpture._Singapore._%288069678494%29.jpg)

*Another sculpture from the "People of the River" series along the Singapore River, depicting the merchants who once traded here. (Photo: Bernard Spragg. NZ, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

### The name today

![The exterior of a restaurant at 70 Boat Quay lit up at night, with diners and the CBD skyline behind](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/BoatQuay%27sGastronomicDelights_16.jpg/1280px-BoatQuay%27sGastronomicDelights_16.jpg)

*Boat Quay at night today — the name survives, advertising restaurants instead of the cargo trade it was built for. (Photo: Damientan14, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.)*
