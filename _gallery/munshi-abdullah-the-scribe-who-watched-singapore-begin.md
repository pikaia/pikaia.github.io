More pictures of the places, people and objects in Munshi Abdullah's account of early Singapore.

### Drawings of the stone's fragments, 1848

![Ink drawings of three broken pieces of stone, each covered in rows of carved letters](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/88/SingaporeStone-drawings.jpg)

*Drawings of three fragments of the inscribed slab, made in 1848 after they reached Calcutta. The bottom one is the piece now known as the Singapore Stone. (Possibly J. W. Laidlay, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A copy of the inscription, 1837

![A sheet of hand-copied characters from a worn inscription, arranged in uneven rows with notes in English](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/1837-SingaporeStone-WBlanddrawing.jpg/1280px-1837-SingaporeStone-WBlanddrawing.jpg)

*James Prinsep's lithograph of a transcription of the inscription made by Dr William Bland in 1837, six years before the slab was blown up. (William Bland, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The Singapore Stone today

![A worn slab of sandstone lit from above in a dark museum case](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/Singapore_Stone_-_20121019.jpg/1280px-Singapore_Stone_-_20121019.jpg)

*The surviving fragment of the Singapore Stone on display at the National Museum of Singapore in 2012. (Bryn Pinzgauer, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Rocky Point on an 1825 survey

![A hand-drawn survey map with a winding river, hills shown by shading and a pointed headland at the river mouth](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Part_of_Singapore_Island_%28British_Library_India_Office_Records%2C_1825%2C_detail%29.jpg/1280px-Part_of_Singapore_Island_%28British_Library_India_Office_Records%2C_1825%2C_detail%29.jpg)

*Part of an 1825 survey map of Singapore, showing the Singapore River and Rocky Point, where the inscribed slab stood. (Unknown author, British Library India Office Records, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Singapore from Government Hill, 1830

![A wide engraving of a low-lying town of scattered houses and trees between a hill and the sea, with ships offshore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/View_of_Singapore_1830_Sophia_Raffles%27_memoir_of_Stamford_Raffles.jpg/1280px-View_of_Singapore_1830_Sophia_Raffles%27_memoir_of_Stamford_Raffles.jpg)

*A view of Singapore looking towards the sea from Government Hill, now Fort Canning, published in Sophia Raffles's memoir of her husband. (Sophia Raffles, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A manuscript copy of the Hikayat, 1843

![An open handwritten book with neat lines of Jawi script on both pages](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7b/AbdullahbinAbdulKadir-HikayatAbdullah-1843.jpg)

*A manuscript copy of the Hikayat Abdullah from 1843, written in Jawi script, now in the Library of Congress. (Abdullah bin Abdul Kadir, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Farquhar in uniform

### The seal of Sultan Hussein, 1819

![A round black seal with Arabic script inside a decorated border](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/21/The_Seal_of_the_Sultan_Hussein_Shah_of_Johor_and_Singapore.jpg)

*The seal of Sultan Hussein Shah of Johor and Singapore, dated 1819, the Sultan to whom Raffles put his questions about Malay law after the attack on Farquhar. (Sultan Hussein Shah of Johor and Singapore, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A Malay manuscript copied in 1824

![Two facing manuscript pages with Jawi text inside richly painted gold and blue borders](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Initial_pages_of_the_Taj_al-Salatin%2C_%E2%80%98The_Crown_of_Kings%E2%80%99%2C_a_Malay_%E2%80%98mirror_for_princes%E2%80%99.jpg/1280px-Initial_pages_of_the_Taj_al-Salatin%2C_%E2%80%98The_Crown_of_Kings%E2%80%99%2C_a_Malay_%E2%80%98mirror_for_princes%E2%80%99.jpg)

*The opening pages of a copy of the Taj al-Salatin, a Malay guide for rulers, made in 1824, the kind of Malay manuscript that Raffles bought in large numbers. (Muhammad bin Umar Syaikh Farid (scribe), CC0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

![A red colonial building with a clock tower in a square, with a red church behind it](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Malakka_Stadhuis.jpg/1280px-Malakka_Stadhuis.jpg)
