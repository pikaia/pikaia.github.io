Two more denominations from opposite ends of the currency's short, inflated life.

### One cent, 1942

![A 1-cent Japanese Occupation note, front and back](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a8/MAL-M1b-Malaya-Japanese_Occupation-One_Cent_ND_%281942%29.jpg)

*A 1-cent note from 1942 — the smallest denomination issued, plain enough that it barely needed a design at all. (Photo: National Numismatic Collection, National Museum of American History, Smithsonian Institution, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### One hundred dollars, 1944

![A $100 Japanese Occupation note from 1944](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9d/One_hundred_dollar_note_issued_by_the_Japanese_Government_during_the_occupation_of_Malaya%2C_North_Borneo%2C_Sarawak_and_Brunei_%281944%2C_obverse%29.jpg)

*A $100 note from 1944 — ten times the currency's original top denomination, and still not the end of it. (Photo: Jacklee, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The same trick, across the empire

Malaya's "banana money" wasn't a one-off. Japan printed unbacked invasion currency for nearly every territory it occupied, and each one inflated the same way.

### Ten pesos, the Philippines, 1942

![A 10-peso Japanese Occupation note for the Philippines, front and back](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2b/PHI-108-Japanese_Government_%28Philippines%29-10_Pesos_%281942%29.jpg)

*A 10-peso note issued for the occupied Philippines, 1942. (Photo: National Numismatic Collection, National Museum of American History, Smithsonian Institution, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Ten rupees, Burma, 1942-44

![A 10-rupee Japanese Occupation note for Burma, front and back](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/09/BUR-16-Japanese_occupation_Burma-10_rupees_%281942-44%29.jpg)

*A 10-rupee note issued for occupied Burma, 1942-44, with a Shwedagon-style pagoda vignette. (Photo: National Numismatic Collection, National Museum of American History, Smithsonian Institution, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Ten roepiah, the Dutch East Indies, 1944

![A 10-roepiah Japanese Occupation note for the Dutch East Indies, front and back](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b5/NI-131-Imperial_Japanese_Government-10_Roepiah_%281944%29.jpg)

*A 10-roepiah note issued for the occupied Dutch East Indies, 1944 — text in Malay rather than Dutch, part of Japan's pitch that it was liberating the islands from colonial rule. (Photo: National Numismatic Collection, National Museum of American History, Smithsonian Institution, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*
