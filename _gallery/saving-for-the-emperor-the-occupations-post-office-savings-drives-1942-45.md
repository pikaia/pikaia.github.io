More newspaper pages, stamps and pictures from the savings drives of 1942 to 1945, and from what happened to the deposits afterwards.

### The first million, August 1943

![A full newspaper page of closely set columns, with a headline near the top right about people's confidence in the Postal Savings Bank](/assets/images/syonan-shimbun-1943-08-23-page-2.jpg)

*The Syonan Shimbun of 23 August 1943, page 2. The report near the top right, "Peoples Confidence In Postal Savings Bank Demonstrated", announces that postal savings in Malaya had passed $1 million and sets out the first Post Office Savings Bank Promotion Week. (The Syonan Shimbun, via NewspaperSG, National Library Board Singapore, public domain)*

### A prize for every $100, February 1944

![A full newspaper page with a photograph at the top and many columns, including an item headed Special $10,000 Prize, Other Bonuses Provided In Banks' Scheme To Foster Savings](/assets/images/syonan-shimbun-1944-02-08-page-2.jpg)

*The Syonan Shimbun of 8 February 1944, page 2. The item at the right, "Special $10,000 Prize, Other Bonuses Provided In Banks' Scheme To Foster Savings", sets out the Malai Banking Association's first "Fixed Deposit with Bonus" scheme. (The Syonan Shimbun, via NewspaperSG, National Library Board Singapore, public domain)*

### A savings week for the "Birth of New Malai", February 1944

![A full newspaper page with a headline at the top left about a new postal savings week, beside small drawings of a stamp and a round date stamp](/assets/images/syonan-shimbun-1944-02-10-page-2.jpg)

*The Syonan Shimbun of 10 February 1944, page 2. The report at the top left, "New Postal Savings Week Being Launched Tuesday", shows the commemorative 8-cent stamp and the date stamp of a father and son walking to the Syonan Jinja. (The Syonan Shimbun, via NewspaperSG, National Library Board Singapore, public domain)*

### Half a million depositors, July 1945

![A full newspaper page with a masthead and many columns of text, including a report on the postal savings bank's figures for June](/assets/images/syonan-shimbun-1945-07-25-page-2.jpg)

*The Syonan Shimbun of 25 July 1945, page 2, three weeks before the surrender. Its report on the savings bank's June figures gives the totals at the end of June 1945: $30.2 million in deposits and 506,950 depositors across Malaya. (The Syonan Shimbun, via NewspaperSG, National Library Board Singapore, public domain)*

### The savings bank reopens, October 1945

![A full newspaper page of text columns and cinema advertisements, including a short item headed Savings Bank Opens On Monday](/assets/images/straits-times-1945-10-12-page-4.jpg)

*The Straits Times of 12 October 1945, page 4. The short item "Savings Bank Opens On Monday", in the middle column, says that no withdrawals or enquiries could be entertained for accounts started during the Japanese occupation. (Singapore Press Holdings, via NewspaperSG, National Library Board Singapore, public domain)*

### Revalued claims, May 1951

![A full newspaper page with large cigarette, chocolate and car advertisements and a short item headed Revalued Savings Claims near the bottom](/assets/images/straits-times-1951-05-09-page-4.jpg)

*The Straits Times of 9 May 1951, page 4. The short item "Revalued Savings Claims", near the bottom, tells depositors how to claim the revaluation of withdrawals made from their pre-war accounts between 1943 and 1945. (Singapore Press Holdings, via NewspaperSG, National Library Board Singapore, public domain)*

### A rubber tapper, 1943

![A black postage stamp showing a man tapping a rubber tree, with Japanese characters along the top and the value 3 cents](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/Stamp_Malaya_Japan_occupation_1943_3c.jpg)

*A 4-cent stamp of the same 1943 issue. (Japanese military administration, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The Syonan Jinja on a stamp

![A violet postage stamp showing a wooden shrine building among trees, with Japanese characters along the top and the value 15 cents](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8f/%E6%98%AD%E5%8D%97%E7%A5%9E%E7%A4%BE%E9%82%AE%E7%A5%A8%EF%BC%88%E6%97%A5%E6%B2%BB%E9%A9%AC%E6%9D%A5%EF%BC%89.jpg)

*A 15-cent stamp showing the Syonan Jinja, the shrine that also appeared on the savings week date stamp of February 1944. (Japanese military administration, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The fall of Singapore stamps, 1942

![Two postage stamps, one red and one green, each showing the portrait of a bearded Japanese admiral with Japanese characters](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f0/1942_Fall_of_Singapore_for_Japanese_stamps.JPG)

*Japan's stamps marking the fall of Singapore in 1942, sold with a surcharge of 1 and 2 sen. The anniversary of the fall, which the administration called the "Birth of New Malai", was used to launch savings drives in February 1944 and February 1945. (Ministry of Communications, Japan, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### "Dai Nippon 2602"

![Two green Malayan revenue stamps overprinted in black with the words Dai Nippon 2602 and a purple handstamp](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/17/3d-Malaya-DutyStamps_SIN-20171213.jpg)

*Pre-war Malayan revenue stamps overprinted "Dai Nippon 2602", the Japanese calendar year for 1942, and used during the Occupation. (Revenue Department of Malaya, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A $10 note

![A green Japanese occupation ten-dollar note with a picture of a fruit tree and a banana plant](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Banknote_%28AM_2004.35.4-1%29.jpg/1280px-Banknote_%28AM_2004.35.4-1%29.jpg)

### Rationed rice

![A buff-coloured ration card printed in Chinese and Malay with handwritten entries in a grid of boxes](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/RationBook-ExtraRice_SIN_1945_20171213.jpg/1280px-RationBook-ExtraRice_SIN_1945_20171213.jpg)
