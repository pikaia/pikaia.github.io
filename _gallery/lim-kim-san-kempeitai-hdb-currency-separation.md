Four more images tracing Lim Kim San's life — his wedding, the college where he met a future ally, the town his HDB is best remembered for, and a monument to the occupation years that shaped him.

### Lim and Pang Gek Kim on their wedding day, 1940

![Lim Kim San with his wife Pang Gek Kim on their wedding day, 1940](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b4/Lim_Kim_San%2C_1940.jpg)

*Lim Kim San with his wife Pang Gek Kim on their wedding day, 24 February 1940, dressed in traditional Peranakan attire. (Photo: National Archives of Singapore, via Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### Raffles College, where he met Goh Keng Swee

![Graduates of Raffles College, 1934](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/28/Raffles_College_graduates_1934.webp)

*Graduates of Raffles College, 1934 — five years before Lim's own graduating class of 1939, where he studied economics alongside a young Goh Keng Swee, later his negotiating partner in the 1967 currency split. (Photo: Eriko Ogihara-Schuck, via Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### Queenstown, the flagship of Lim's HDB

![Thirty-storey HDB flats along Strathmore Avenue in Queenstown, Singapore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5c/Queenstown_hdb.jpg)

*HDB flats along Strathmore Avenue in Queenstown, the satellite town built as the flagship project of Lim's HDB chairmanship, photographed in 2004. (Photo: Calvin Teo / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.5)*

### A memorial to the years that shaped him

![The Civilian War Memorial, Singapore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/27/Civilian_War_Memorial%2C_Singapore-3276.jpg)

*The Civilian War Memorial, unveiled in 1967, commemorating civilians killed during the Japanese Occupation — the same years in which Lim was arrested and tortured by the Kempeitai. (Photo: Bijay Chaurasia / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*
