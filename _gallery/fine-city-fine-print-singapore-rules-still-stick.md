Three more images tracing the era that produced Singapore's "fine city" rules — the government that passed them, and the crowded, informal street life they were written to clean up.

### The government behind the 1968 clean-up law

![Lee Kuan Yew, 1965](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/13/Lee_Kuan_Yew%2C_1965_%28cropped%29.jpg)

*Prime Minister Lee Kuan Yew in 1965, three years before his government passed the Environmental Public Health Act that still underpins today's littering and toilet-flushing fines. (Photo: Australian News and Information Bureau, Canberra / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### The conditions the law was written for

![A kampong in Braddell Hill, Singapore, about 1964](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/81/Kampong_in_Braddell_Hill_Singapore_about_1964.jpg)

*A kampong at Braddell Hill, Singapore, around 1964 — the kind of crowded, informally-serviced settlement the 1968 public health push was aimed at cleaning up. (Photo: Don Christie / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### Informal street life, pre-crackdown

![A street vendor in Singapore, 1973-74](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ef/Singapore-Street_Vendor_1973-74-WUS08155.jpg)

*A street vendor in Singapore, 1973-74 — the kind of unlicensed, informal street commerce that Singapore's public-order campaigns spent the following decades regulating and cleaning up. (Photo: Rainer Halama / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*
