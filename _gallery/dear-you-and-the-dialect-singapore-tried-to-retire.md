Five more images from the dialect-speaking Singapore that the Speak Mandarin Campaign was built to phase out — its opera stages, its amusement parks, its street hawkers, and its clan temples.

### A Chinese theatre in the Straits Settlements

![Chinese theater in the Straits Settlements, circa 1900](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/KITLV_-_106226_-_Lambert_%26_Co.%2C_G.R._-_Singapore_-_Chinese_theater_in_the_Straits_Settlements_-_circa_1900.tif/lossy-page1-960px-KITLV_-_106226_-_Lambert_%26_Co.%2C_G.R._-_Singapore_-_Chinese_theater_in_the_Straits_Settlements_-_circa_1900.tif.jpg)

*A Chinese theatre in the Straits Settlements, photographed by G.R. Lambert & Co. around 1900 — this kind of dialect opera, performed in Hokkien, Teochew or Cantonese depending on the troupe, was mainstream entertainment before the campaign years. (Photo: Lambert & Co., KITLV collection / Leiden University Library / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### Lai Chun Yuen, Chinatown's Cantonese opera house

![Former Lai Chun Yuen opera theatre building, Trengganu Street, Singapore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Former_Lai_Chun_Yuen%2C_Singapore.jpg/960px-Former_Lai_Chun_Yuen%2C_Singapore.jpg)

*The former Lai Chun Yuen at the junction of Trengganu Street and Smith Street, Chinatown — built in 1887 as one of Singapore's earliest dedicated Chinese opera theatres, staging Cantonese opera for a dialect-speaking audience. (Photo: ashish100 / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0)*

### Great World, where dialect entertainment drew crowds

![Entrance to Great World Amusement Park, Singapore, circa 1950](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Great_World_Amusement_Park.jpg/500px-Great_World_Amusement_Park.jpg)

*The entrance to Great World amusement park, circa 1950. Great World and its rivals New World and Gay World hosted dialect opera and getai performances that drew nightly crowds in the decades before the Speak Mandarin Campaign. (Photo: National Archives of Singapore / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### The hawker banter the census could still measure

![Chinese street vendor in Singapore, circa 1890](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/10/KITLV_-_103776_-_Chinese_street_vendor_in_Singapore_-_circa_1890.tif/lossy-page1-960px-KITLV_-_103776_-_Chinese_street_vendor_in_Singapore_-_circa_1890.tif.jpg)

*A Chinese street vendor in Singapore, circa 1890 — the kind of hawker's banter, once conducted entirely in dialect, that today's Singaporeans in their 40s and 50s can often still follow without being able to hold a full conversation in return. (Photo: KITLV collection / Leiden University Library / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### A Chinese temple, the clan's anchor

![Chinese temple in Singapore, 1895, identified as Thian Hock Keng](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/KITLV_-_29181_-_Chinese_temple_in_Singapore_-_1895.tif/lossy-page1-960px-KITLV_-_29181_-_Chinese_temple_in_Singapore_-_1895.tif.jpg)

*A Chinese temple in Singapore, 1895 — temples like this one anchored the dialect and clan associations that organised Chinese community life before Mandarin became the common tongue. (Photo: KITLV collection / Leiden University Library / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*
