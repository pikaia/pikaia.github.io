Six more images from the Singapore today's oldest generation grew up and grew old in — the informal trades they worked, the housing that replaced the kampong, the campaign that shrank the large families many once counted on for old age, and the fitness corners that are part of daily life today.

### A fitness corner by the water

![Fitness corner along East Coast Parkway, Singapore](/assets/images/east_cost_parkway.jpeg)

*A public fitness corner along East Coast Parkway — the same kind of exercise stations found across Singapore's estates and parks, here set against the Strait of Singapore. (Photo: Chris Lee)*

### A street vendor's trade, 1973/74

![Street vendor in Singapore, 1973/74](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ef/Singapore-Street_Vendor_1973-74-WUS08155.jpg)

*A street vendor in Singapore, photographed in 1973/74 — the kind of informal trade many of today's oldest Singaporeans relied on for a living before the retirement system caught up with them. (Photo: Rainer Halama / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### A hawker centre in the 1970s

![Hawker centre in Singapore, 1973/74](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6d/Singapore-Hawker_Centre.1973-74-WUS08151.jpg)

*A Singapore hawker centre photographed in 1973/74, decades before today's seniors would return to stalls like these for light work in old age. (Photo: Rainer Halama / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### Building the housing that replaced the kampong

![Public housing construction in Singapore, 1973/74](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/87/Singapore-Public_Housing-1973-74-WUS08215.jpg)

*Public housing under construction in Singapore, 1973/74 — the HDB blocks that would eventually replace extended-family kampongs as most Singaporeans' address. (Photo: Rainer Halama / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### "Please stop at two"

![Singapore family planning campaign poster, photographed 1973](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Family_planning%2C_Singapore_-_UNESCO_-_PHOTO0000000902_0001.tiff/lossy-page1-960px-Family_planning%2C_Singapore_-_UNESCO_-_PHOTO0000000902_0001.tiff.jpg)

*A Singapore family-planning campaign poster, photographed in 1973 for UNESCO — the "Stop at Two" drive that helped shrink the large families many older Singaporeans had once counted on as their retirement plan. (Photo: Dominique Roger / UNESCO / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO)*

### An HDB living room from the era

![Recreated HDB flat living room from the 1970s and 1980s, National Museum of Singapore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/15/HDB_living_room_%281970s_and_1980s%29%2C_Singapore_History_Gallery%2C_National_Museum_of_Singapore_-_20151125-01.jpg)

*A recreated HDB flat living room from the 1970s and 1980s, on display at the Singapore History Gallery, National Museum of Singapore — the kind of home many of today's seniors raised their families in. (Photo: Jacklee / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*
