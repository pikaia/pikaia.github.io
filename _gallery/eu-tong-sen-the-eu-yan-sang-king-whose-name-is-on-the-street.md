More photographs of Eu Tong Sen Street, its theatres and hotels, the Eu Yan Sang buildings, and the firm's shops across the region.

### The street and its theatres

![Eu Tong Sen Street in 2025](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/20/Eu_Tong_Sen_Street%2C_Singapore%2C_2025_%2801%29.jpg)

*Eu Tong Sen Street at the Yue Hwa junction, looking along the road that took his name in 1919. (Bahnfrend, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2025)*

![Eu Tong Sen Street and New Bridge Road at night](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5d/Eu_Tong_Sen_Street_%2B_New_Bridge_Road%2C_Singapore%2C_2014_%2801%29.JPG)

*The split of Eu Tong Sen Street and New Bridge Road at night — the two roads run parallel through Chinatown and merge at the carriageway built in the late 1980s. (Bahnfrend, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2014)*

![The Majestic in 2005](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/20/The_Majestic%2C_Dec_05.JPG)

*The Majestic Theatre in 2005, two years after it reopened as a shopping mall behind its conserved 1927 façade. (Sengkang, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2005)*

![The Majestic in 2025](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/35/Chinatown-SG_-_The_Majestic_%282025%29_-_img_01.jpg)

*The Majestic in 2025 — the glazed tiles, blue dragons and opera figures of Eu's Cantonese opera house, kept through every change of use since. (Chainwit., CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2025)*

### The buildings, then and now

![The Yue Hwa Building in 2005](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/15/Yue_Hwa_Building%2C_Dec_05.JPG)

*The Yue Hwa Building in 2005 — built in 1927 as the Nam Tin Building and the Great Southern Hotel, and claimed for Eu by some National Library Board accounts and for Lum Chang Holdings by others. (Sengkang, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2005)*

![The Yue Hwa store frontage in 2010](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/37/Yue_Hwa_Chinese_Products_Singapore_Store.jpg)

*The Yue Hwa Chinese Products storefront in 2010, under the building's 裕華國貨 signage. (WiNG, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2010)*

![The Eu Yan Sang Building in 2025](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6f/Chinatown-SG_-_Old_Eu_Yan_Sang_Building%2C_South_Bridge_Road%2C_Singapore_%282025%29_-_img_01.jpg)

*The old Eu Yan Sang Building on South Bridge Road in 2025, restored after the group's 2019 sale of the lease and reopened as a co-working space. (Chainwit., CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2025)*

![Eu Yan Sang headquarters in 2010](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/44/Eu_Yan_Sang_Headquarters_2010.jpg)

*The South Bridge Road building as the group's headquarters in 2010, before the move to Tai Seng Drive in 2013. (WiNG, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2010)*
