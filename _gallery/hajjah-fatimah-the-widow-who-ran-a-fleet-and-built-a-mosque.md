Thirteen more images: the Singapore waterfront in Hajjah Fatimah's time, and the mosque she built on Beach Road as it looks today.

### The Strait of Singapore, surveyed 1846-54

![An Admiralty sea chart of the Strait of Singapore, with depth soundings, the coast of Singapore island and the islands to the south](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Admiralty_Chart_No_2404_Strait_of_Singapore_Sheet_3_Surveyed_by_J.T._Thomson_Esq._Government_Survr._1846-54%2C_Published_1855%2C_Corrections_to_1866.jpg/1280px-Admiralty_Chart_No_2404_Strait_of_Singapore_Sheet_3_Surveyed_by_J.T._Thomson_Esq._Government_Survr._1846-54%2C_Published_1855%2C_Corrections_to_1866.jpg)

*Admiralty Chart No. 2404, from the survey of the strait by John Turnbull Thomson, the Government Surveyor, in 1846-54, the years just after the mosque was built. (United Kingdom Hydrographic Office, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Junks at anchor, 1841

![A pencil-style lithograph of two large Siamese junks at anchor, with smaller boats around them](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Junks_of_Siam_at_Anchor_in_Singapore_1841_Francois-Edmond_Paris.png/1280px-Junks_of_Siam_at_Anchor_in_Singapore_1841_Francois-Edmond_Paris.png)

*"Junks of Siam at Anchor in Singapore", a lithograph by the French naval officer François-Edmond Pâris, 1841. Trading ships from across the region crowded the harbour in Fatimah's day. (François-Edmond Pâris, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A trading junk in the harbour

![An engraving of a large Chinese junk under sail, with other ships behind it](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Atlas_pittoresque_pl_136.jpg/1280px-Atlas_pittoresque_pl_136.jpg)
