The doctor-reformer's other portraits, his friend's great book, and the places that still carry his name — Xiamen's campus, Bidadari's garden, and the station.

### The president's portrait

![Lim Boon Keng in a formal portrait from a Xiamen University commemorative volume](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cd/%E6%9E%97%E6%96%87%E6%85%B6.jpg)

*Lim Boon Keng in Western dress, from a commemorative volume of Xiamen University — the institution he led, without salary, from 1921 to 1937. (Xiamen University commemorative publication, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The book that remembers them all

![Title page of One Hundred Years' History of the Chinese in Singapore, 1923](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1f/One_Hundred_Years%27_History_of_the_Chinese_in_Singapore.png)

*The title page of Song Ong Siang's One Hundred Years' History of the Chinese in Singapore (1923) — the chronicle of their community, written by the schoolboy who outscored Lim in 1887 and built beside him for fifty years. (Song Ong Siang, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Sun Yat-sen in 1910

![Sun Yat-sen in 1910](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/38/Sun_Yat-sen_1910.jpg)

*Sun Yat-sen in 1910, a year before the revolution that made Lim his personal physician and confidential secretary, and put him in charge of the new republic's health services. (Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The gate of Bidadari

![The relocated gate of the former Bidadari Cemetery at Bidadari Memorial Garden](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/22/Gate_of_the_former_Bidadari_Cemetery%2C_Bidadari_Garden%2C_Singapore_-_20121008-01.jpg)

*The gate of the old Bidadari Cemetery, kept at the Bidadari Memorial Garden at Mount Vernon. Lim was buried behind this gate in 1957; when the cemetery was cleared, his tombstone was among the few moved into the garden. (Smuconlaw, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2012)*

### The station that bears his name

![Boon Keng MRT station entrance](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2a/Boon_Keng_MRT_Station.jpg)

*Boon Keng MRT station (NE9) on the North East Line, opened on 20 June 2003 — the name of the Grand Old Man of Singapore, carried now by a station signboard. (Fans018, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*
