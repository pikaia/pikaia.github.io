Seven more images tracing the officials and institutions behind Singapore's employment and retirement-savings policy — the people and buildings behind the rules the ageism gap now runs up against.

### Devan Nair, who built the modern labour movement

![Devan Nair with his wife Avadai Dhanam, 1953](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f6/Devan_Nair%2C_1953_%283x4_crop%29.png)

*Devan Nair, photographed with his wife Avadai Dhanam upon his release from prison in 1953. He went on to serve as Secretary-General of the National Trades Union Congress from 1970 to 1979, shaping the tripartite labour movement that still negotiates wage and re-employment guidelines today. (Photo: The Straits Times / Wikimedia Commons, public domain in Singapore)*

### Jek Yeun Thong, Singapore's first Minister for Labour

![Jek Yeun Thong, Singapore's Minister for Labour, in 1964](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/26/Jek_Yeun_Thong%2C_1964.jpg)

*Jek Yeun Thong, who served as Minister for Labour from 1963 to 1968, meeting Australian politician Paul Hasluck at the Australian Institute of Management in Canberra in 1964. His ministry laid the groundwork for the Employment Act 1968, the law still underpinning Singapore's workplace regulation. (Photo: Australian News and Information Bureau, Canberra / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### S. Rajaratnam, who took over the labour portfolio in 1968

![S. Rajaratnam, c. 1940s](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/09/S_Rajaratnam_c._1940s.jpg)

*S. Rajaratnam, photographed decades before he succeeded Jek Yeun Thong as Minister for Labour — later renamed Minister for Manpower — on 16 April 1968. (Photo: National Archives of Singapore / Wikimedia Commons, public domain in Singapore)*

### The CPF Building, retirement savings' former headquarters

![The CPF Building on Robinson Road, Singapore, 2006](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/63/CPF_Building%2C_Jan_06.JPG)

*The CPF Building on Robinson Road, headquarters of the Central Provident Fund Board — the retirement savings scheme every one of Singapore's older jobseekers depends on — until it was sold in 2015 and later demolished. (Photo: Sengkang / Wikimedia Commons, copyrighted free use)*

### The building where Singapore's labour policy was made

![The former Ministry of Labour Building, Singapore, 2025](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a3/Former_Ministry_of_Labour_Building%2C_October_2025.jpg)

*The former Ministry of Labour Building on Havelock Square, built in 1928 and gazetted a national monument in 1997. It housed the ministry that shaped Singapore's early labour law, before the portfolio moved on and the building became the Family Justice Courts. (Photo: GoAheadFan95 / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### Where the labour portfolio sits today

![The Ministry of Manpower building on Havelock Road, Singapore, 2006](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/97/Ministry_of_Manpower.JPG)

*The Ministry of Manpower building on Havelock Road — successor to the old Ministry of Labour Building just up the street. MOM sets the retirement and re-employment ages the post describes, raising them again from July 2026. (Photo: Terence Ong / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.5)*

### The labour movement's headquarters now

![The NTUC Centre at One Marina Boulevard, Singapore, 2006](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ae/NTUC_Centre.JPG)

*The NTUC Centre at One Marina Boulevard, headquarters of the National Trades Union Congress — the labour movement Devan Nair helped build, and one of the three partners behind the 2023 employer survey in the post. (Photo: Terence Ong / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.5)*
