More on Elizabeth Choy, the people around her, and the places tied to her story.

### With her daughters, 1953

![Elizabeth Choy in a floral qipao, holding one of her young daughters, with another child beside her](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/79/Elizabeth_Choy%2C_1953.png)

*Elizabeth Choy in 1953, with two of her three adopted daughters. Her qipaos and bangles earned her the nickname "the Dayak Woman of Singapore." (Singapore Press Holdings, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

### Those she was risking her life for

![Liberated Allied prisoners of war lying in a corridor and looking out from cell doorways at Changi Prison, 1945](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f2/Allied_prisoners_of_war_after_the_liberation_of_Changi_Prison%2C_Singapore_-_c._1945.jpg)

*Liberated Allied prisoners of war at Changi Prison, 1945 — the internees Elizabeth and her husband spent years secretly supplying with food, medicine, money and messages. (Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

### Cast in bronze

![A bronze sculpture of a reclining nude figure holding a child, displayed on a plinth in a gallery](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/50/Serene_Jade_%281949%29_by_Dora_Gordine%2C_National_Gallery_Singapore_-_20160101.jpg/1280px-Serene_Jade_%281949%29_by_Dora_Gordine%2C_National_Gallery_Singapore_-_20160101.jpg)

*"Serene Jade" (1949), sculpted by Dora Gordine with Elizabeth Choy as her model during Choy's years recuperating in England — made because Choy couldn't afford art school herself, and posed for one instead. On display at the National Gallery Singapore. (Photo: Jacklee, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

### Across the road from where it happened

![The National Museum of Singapore's colonial-era facade, white with a large dome, seen from across the street](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/National_Museum_of_Singapore%2C_Stamford_Road%2C_October_2025.jpg/1280px-National_Museum_of_Singapore%2C_Stamford_Road%2C_October_2025.jpg)

*The National Museum of Singapore, directly across the road from the YMCA site where Elizabeth Choy was held and tortured — one of the most-visited buildings in Singapore, next to one of the least-noticed markers. (Photo: GoAheadFan95, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.)*
