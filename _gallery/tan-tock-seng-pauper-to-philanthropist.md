Eight more images from Tan Tock Seng's Singapore — the river where he made his first fortune, the Telok Ayer streetscape and temple he helped build, the colonial offices and Pearl's Hill prison that shaped the hospital's early years, and the son who carried his philanthropy forward after he died.

### Tan Kim Ching, the son who carried it on

![Portrait of Tan Kim Ching](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cc/Tan_Kim_Ching.jpg)

*Tan Kim Ching, Tan Tock Seng's eldest son, who kept funding the hospital after his father's death and went on to serve as president of Thian Hock Keng for over 40 years. Published in Song Ong Siang's* One Hundred Years' History of the Chinese in Singapore *(1923). (Source: Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### Thian Hock Keng, the temple he helped found

![Thian Hock Keng Temple, Singapore, circa 1890](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/KITLV_-_103742_-_Lambert_%26_Co._-_Thian_Hock_Keng_Temple_in_Singapore_-_circa_1890.tif/lossy-page1-960px-KITLV_-_103742_-_Lambert_%26_Co._-_Thian_Hock_Keng_Temple_in_Singapore_-_circa_1890.tif.jpg)

*Thian Hock Keng Temple on Telok Ayer Street, photographed by G.R. Lambert & Co. around 1890. Tan Tock Seng was among its founding donors. (Photo: Lambert & Co., KITLV collection / Leiden University Library / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### Telok Ayer, the Hokkien community's street

![Telok Ayer Market, Singapore, drawn by J.T. Thomson in 1847](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/77/Telok_Ayer_Market_-_J.T._Thomson.jpg)

*Telok Ayer Market, drawn by government surveyor John Turnbull Thomson in 1847 — the waterfront market street where the Hokkien community, and Tan Tock Seng's own trade, was concentrated. (Image: John Turnbull Thomson, 1847 / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### Boat Quay, where the fortune began

![Panorama of the Singapore River from Boat Quay, 1860](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/KITLV_-_29175_-_View_of_the_harbor_of_Singapore_-_1860.tif/lossy-page1-960px-KITLV_-_29175_-_View_of_the_harbor_of_Singapore_-_1860.tif.jpg)

*The Singapore River seen from Boat Quay in 1860 — the stretch of waterfront where a young Tan Tock Seng opened the shop that made his fortune. (Photo: KITLV collection / Leiden University Library / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### The harbour his trade depended on

![Singapore harbour, January 1866, engraving by Frederick Grosse](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/07/Singapore_January_20_1866_Frederick_Grosse.jpg)

*An 1866 engraving of Singapore's harbour by Frederick Grosse, showing the port town whose maritime trade underpinned Tan Tock Seng's land and shipping fortune. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### The shophouses of the riverfront

![Shophouses along the Singapore River](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Shophouses_aan_de_Singapore-rivier_te_Singapore%2C_KITLV_156146.tiff/lossy-page1-960px-Shophouses_aan_de_Singapore-rivier_te_Singapore%2C_KITLV_156146.tiff.jpg)

*Shophouses along the Singapore River, from the KITLV collection. Tan owned shophouse property of exactly this kind, including the Ellenborough Building. (Photo: KITLV collection / Leiden University Library / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0)*

### The colonial government's offices

![Public Offices, Singapore, drawn by J.T. Thomson in 1846](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/df/Public_Offices%2C_Singapore_by_John_Turnbull_Thomson_1846.jpg)

*The Public Offices in Singapore, drawn by John Turnbull Thomson in 1846 — the seat of the colonial administration that spent two decades failing to build a hospital for the poor, then proposed taxing the Chinese community to pay for Tan's. (Image: John Turnbull Thomson, 1846 / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### Pearl's Hill, and the prison that displaced the hospital

![Outram Prison on Pearl's Hill, Singapore, photographed in the 1850s](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/54/Photograph_of_Outram_Prison_%28Pearl%E2%80%99s_Hill_Prison%29_in_the_1850%27s.jpg)

*Outram Prison on Pearl's Hill, photographed in the 1850s. The hospital's first building, finished in 1846 on the same hill, was used as a convict jail while a proper prison was built — leaving the sick in an attap shed at the foot of the hill until a storm destroyed it in 1849. (Photo: unknown photographer, c.1850s / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*
