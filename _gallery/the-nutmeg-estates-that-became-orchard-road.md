More on the plantations, the planters and the street names they left behind.

### A plantation house, painted in 1842

![A watercolour landscape of a country house on rolling hills at Cairnhill, Singapore, surrounded by scattered trees, painted in 1842](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Charles_Andrew_Dyce%2C_Cairnhill%2C_Singapore%2C_1842%2C_Watercolour_%26_ink_on_paper%2C_263_x_363_mm.png/1280px-Charles_Andrew_Dyce%2C_Cairnhill%2C_Singapore%2C_1842%2C_Watercolour_%26_ink_on_paper%2C_263_x_363_mm.png)

*Cairnhill, Singapore, painted by Charles Andrew Dyce in 1842 — a rare contemporary view of one of the actual nutmeg estates, its country house standing where high-rise apartments stand today. (Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

### The planter's grave

![A weathered stone gravestone at Fort Canning Green, Singapore, marking the burial of William Cuppage](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Gravestone_of_William_Cuppage%2C_Fort_Canning_Green%2C_Singapore_-_20130401-01.jpg/1280px-Gravestone_of_William_Cuppage%2C_Fort_Canning_Green%2C_Singapore_-_20130401-01.jpg)

*The gravestone of William Cuppage at Fort Canning Green, Singapore. Cuppage leased and later owned the nutmeg plantation on Emerald Hill, lived there until his death in 1872, and is buried a short walk from the land he planted. (Photo: Smuconlaw, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

### Built on an abandoned plantation

![Burkill Hall, a two-storey colonial bungalow with a wide veranda, at the Singapore Botanic Gardens](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Burkill_Hall_%2843566915180%29.jpg/1280px-Burkill_Hall_%2843566915180%29.jpg)

*Burkill Hall at the Singapore Botanic Gardens, completed in 1868 for the gardens' first superintendent, Lawrence Niven. The Gardens themselves began in 1859 on 23 hectares of abandoned nutmeg plantation, and Burkill Hall is described on its own heritage marker as the last Anglo-Malay plantation house left in Singapore. (Photo: Hugh Llewelyn, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

### Emerald Hill, before the terraces filled in

![A 1973-74 street view of Emerald Hill Road, Singapore, with older low-rise buildings and cars parked along the street](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Singapore-Emerald_Hill_Road-1973-74-WUS08237.jpg/1280px-Singapore-Emerald_Hill_Road-1973-74-WUS08237.jpg)

*Emerald Hill Road, 1973–74, a working street of Peranakan terrace houses decades before conservation status made it a heritage attraction. (Photo: Rainer Halama, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

### The shopping belt today

![ION Orchard's tower and canopy lit up at dusk, with the bronze Nutmeg and Mace sculpture just visible at street level among the trees](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/ION_Orchard.jpg/1280px-ION_Orchard.jpg)

*ION Orchard at dusk. Look closely at street level, among the trees to the left of the entrance, and the reddish curved form of Kumari Nahappan's "Nutmeg and Mace" sculpture is just visible &mdash; a two-tonne bronze marker of exactly the history this post is about, easy to miss at a glance. (Photo: Chensiyuan, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

### The street signs that remember

![A green Singapore street sign reading "Nutmeg Road"](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Nutmeg_Road_2.JPG/1280px-Nutmeg_Road_2.JPG)

*Nutmeg Road, in Novena — one of a handful of Singapore streets still named directly for the spice itself, rather than for a planter. (Photo: Terence Ong, CC BY 2.5, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

![A green Singapore street sign reading "Cuppage Road"](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Cuppage_Road%2C_Singapore_-_20061029.jpg/1280px-Cuppage_Road%2C_Singapore_-_20061029.jpg)

*Cuppage Road, named for William Cuppage's nutmeg plantation on Emerald Hill. (Photo: Terence Ong, CC BY 2.5, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

![A green Singapore street sign reading "Killiney Road"](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Killiney_Road.JPG/1280px-Killiney_Road.JPG)

*Killiney Road, named for Dr Thomas Oxley's Killiney Estate. (Photo: Terence Ong, CC BY 2.5, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

![A green Singapore street sign reading "Claymore Road"](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ad/Claymore_Road.JPG/1280px-Claymore_Road.JPG)

*Claymore Road, named for Captain William Scott's Claymore Estate, once Singapore's third-largest nutmeg plantation. (Photo: Terence Ong, CC BY 2.5, via Wikimedia Commons.)*

![A green Singapore street sign reading "Scotts Road"](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fe/Scotts-Road-Singapore-2006.jpg)

*Scotts Road, the other half of Captain William Scott's namesake — he was Singapore's Harbour Master and Postmaster, and a first cousin of the novelist Sir Walter Scott. (Photo: Kham Tran, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons.)*
