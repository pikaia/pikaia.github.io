Singapore's own LNG terminal is a restricted site, and no freely licensed photograph of it exists — so this gallery shows the rest of the chain it belongs to: the ships that feed it, and the terminals at the other ends of its trade routes.

### The ships

![A Qatargas LNG carrier with four spherical tanks at sea](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f2/Lijmiliya_LNG_carrier_QatarGas.jpg/1280px-Lijmiliya_LNG_carrier_QatarGas.jpg)

*The Lijmiliya, a Q-Flex class carrier of Qatargas, built in South Korea in 2009 — the kind of ship that has carried Qatari cargoes to Singapore since 2013. (devopstom, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

![Two LNG carriers on the water, seen from Sembawang in 2021](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/LNG_Carriers_dilihat_dari_Sembawang_2021.jpg/1280px-LNG_Carriers_dilihat_dari_Sembawang_2021.jpg)

*LNG carriers in Singapore waters, seen from Sembawang in 2021. (Encik Tekateki, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The island it stands on

![A wide panorama across the Jurong industrial estate to Jurong Island](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Jurong_Industrial_Estate_and_Jurong_Island%2C_panorama%2C_Nov_06.jpg/1280px-Jurong_Industrial_Estate_and_Jurong_Island%2C_panorama%2C_Nov_06.jpg)

*Jurong Island behind the Jurong industrial estate in 2006, the year the terminal was decided on — four years before construction began on its south-western shore. (Sengkang, Copyrighted free use, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The export end: where the liquid is made

![A liquefaction plant on an island in daylight, with storage tanks and a jetty](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/00919_NOR_Hammerfest_Melkoya_LNG_Terminal_day_V-P.jpg/1280px-00919_NOR_Hammerfest_Melkoya_LNG_Terminal_day_V-P.jpg)

*Melkøya, Norway, by day — the liquefaction plant that cools gas from the Snøhvit field into liquid for export. (Virtual-Pano, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

![Storage tanks at a Japanese LNG terminal](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Negishi_LNG_Terminal_01.jpg/1280px-Negishi_LNG_Terminal_01.jpg)

*The Negishi terminal in Yokohama, Japan — among the oldest LNG receiving terminals in the world, in service since the first years of the trade. (Σ64, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The import end: Singapore's counterparts

![A jetty with loading pipelines at a Welsh LNG terminal](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Jetty_and_pipelines.jpg/1280px-Jetty_and_pipelines.jpg)

*The jetty and pipelines at South Hook, Wales, where Qatari cargoes are turned back into gas for Britain's network. (David P Howard, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

![Loading arms at an American LNG terminal awaiting a tanker](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/Golden_Pass_LNG%2C_Sabine_Pass.jpg/1280px-Golden_Pass_LNG%2C_Sabine_Pass.jpg)

*Loading arms at Golden Pass, Sabine Pass, Texas. (roy.luck, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

![An LNG terminal with white storage tanks in Poland](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/LNG_terminal%2C_Swinoujscie_%2820250326-P1034516%29.jpg/1280px-LNG_terminal%2C_Swinoujscie_%2820250326-P1034516%29.jpg)

*The Świnoujście terminal in Poland in 2025, built — like Singapore's — to give the country a seaborne alternative to pipeline gas. (Matti Blume, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

![An LNG terminal seen from the water in Maryland](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Cove_Point_LNG_Terminal.jpg/1280px-Cove_Point_LNG_Terminal.jpg)
