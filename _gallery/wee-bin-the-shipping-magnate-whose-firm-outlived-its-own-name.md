Eight more pictures of the Singapore Wee Bin & Co. worked in — the roads and the river in the firm's decades, the lighters that fed its steamers, and the waterfront as it stood in the liquidation years.

### The harbour in 1860

![A wide panorama of the harbour with masts and hulls along the shore and hills behind](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/KITLV_-_29175_-_View_of_the_harbor_of_Singapore_-_1860.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_-_29175_-_View_of_the_harbor_of_Singapore_-_1860.tif.jpg)

*The harbour of Singapore in 1860, four years after Wee Bin founded his firm — a forest of masts along the roads. (KITLV, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The river port about 1870

![Warehouses and moored craft along a riverbank crowded with boats](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/KITLV_-_106514_-_Port_on_the_river_in_Singapore_-_circa_1870.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_-_106514_-_Port_on_the_river_in_Singapore_-_circa_1870.tif.jpg)

*The port on the Singapore River about 1870, just after the founder's death, when his son was learning the business his father had left him. (KITLV, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Tongkangs in the harbour

![Flat-bottomed Chinese lighters with eyes painted on their prows, crowded with cargo](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/Chinese_schepen_%28tongkans%29_in_haven_van_Singapore%2C_RP-F-F01149-FY.jpg/1280px-Chinese_schepen_%28tongkans%29_in_haven_van_Singapore%2C_RP-F-F01149-FY.jpg)

*Chinese tongkangs — the lighters that carried cargo between the steamers at anchor and the godowns, the indispensable middle step of the trade. (Rijksmuseum, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Ships and rowing boats

![Anchored sailing ships and steamers with small rowing boats moving between them](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Schepen_en_roeiboten_in_de_haven_van_Singapore_Harbour%2C_S.pore_%28titel_op_object%29%2C_RP-F-F01197-AE.jpg/1280px-Schepen_en_roeiboten_in_de_haven_van_Singapore_Harbour%2C_S.pore_%28titel_op_object%29%2C_RP-F-F01197-AE.jpg)

*Ships and rowing boats in Singapore harbour — sail and steam side by side, as they were through most of the firm's life. (Rijksmuseum, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### North Boat Quay about 1900

![A postcard view of godowns along the river with Fort Canning rising behind](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b6/Singapore_Boat_Quay_ca._1900.jpg)

*North Boat Quay about 1900, Fort Canning behind — the godown river as Lim Ho Puah's passenger steamers knew it. (Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Collyer Quay about 1910

![A broad quay with godowns, carts and a steamer alongside](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/KITLV_-_79887_-_Kleingrothe%2C_C.J._-_Medan_-_Collyer_Quay%2C_Quay_in_Singapore_-_circa_1910.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_-_79887_-_Kleingrothe%2C_C.J._-_Medan_-_Collyer_Quay%2C_Quay_in_Singapore_-_circa_1910.tif.jpg)

*Collyer Quay about 1910 — the waterfront in the liquidation years, months from the auction of the firm's steamers. (C. J. Kleingrothe, KITLV, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Tanjong Pagar about 1910

![A panoramic view of docks, sheds and moored steamers along a curving shore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/KITLV_-_79886_-_Kleingrothe%2C_C.J._-_Medan_-_Tanjong_Pagar%2C_harbor_in_Singapore_-_circa_1910.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_-_79886_-_Kleingrothe%2C_C.J._-_Medan_-_Tanjong_Pagar%2C_harbor_in_Singapore_-_circa_1910.tif.jpg)

*Tanjong Pagar harbour about 1910 — Lim Ho Puah sat on the board of the Tanjong Pagar Dock Company while he ran the firm. (C. J. Kleingrothe, KITLV, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### The harbour in 1879, in colour

![A watercolour of the harbour at sunset, ships at anchor under a glowing sky](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/47/John_Edmund_Taylor%2C_The_Harbour%2C_Singapore_%281879%2C_Wellcome_V0037490%29.jpg)

*The harbour in 1879, in a watercolour by John Edmund Taylor — the roads at sunset in Boon Teck's years, the pool's one colour picture of the age. (Wellcome Collection, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*
