Fifteen more photographs and prints of the Singapore that P. Govindasamy Pillai arrived in, the South India he left, and the places his business reached.

### Tanjong Pagar, where he landed

![A panoramic view across the harbour to the long wharves and godowns of the Tanjong Pagar Dock Company, with steamers moored alongside, mounted on an album page](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Tanjong_Pagar_in_Singapore_gezien_vanuit_het_westen_Panoramic_view_of_Tanjong_Pagar_from_the_west_%28titel_op_object%29%2C_RP-F-F01140-AE.jpg/1280px-Tanjong_Pagar_in_Singapore_gezien_vanuit_het_westen_Panoramic_view_of_Tanjong_Pagar_from_the_west_%28titel_op_object%29%2C_RP-F-F01140-AE.jpg)

*Tanjong Pagar seen from the west, between about 1890 and 1905. Pillai landed here in 1905. (Rijksmuseum, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Steamers at the wharf

![Three steamships tied up alongside a long wharf with sheds behind, seen across calm water, mounted on an album page](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Drie_schepen_van_de_Glen_Line_aan_de_kade_van_de_Tanjong_Pagar_Dock_Co._Ltd._in_Singapore_Three_glen_steamers_alongside_the_wharf_%28titel_op_object%29%2C_RP-F-F01140-O.jpg/1280px-thumbnail.jpg)

*Three Glen Line steamers alongside the wharf of the Tanjong Pagar Dock Company, between about 1890 and 1905. (Rijksmuseum, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Greetings from Singapore

![A postcard of a large group of Indian men, women and children posed in rows in front of a thatched wall, with the printed words Greetings from Singapore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/KITLV_-_1404957_-_Greetings_from_Singapore_-_1895-1908.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_-_1404957_-_Greetings_from_Singapore_-_1895-1908.tif.jpg)

*A "Greetings from Singapore" postcard of a group of Indian men, women and children, published between 1895 and 1908. (Unknown photographer, KITLV, Leiden University Libraries, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A South Indian family

![A studio photograph of a South Indian man and two women in saris, one young, standing together against a plain backdrop](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/KITLV_-_50187_-_Lambert_%26_Co.%2C_G.R._-_Singapore_-_Klingalese_family_at_Singapore_-_circa_1900.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_-_50187_-_Lambert_%26_Co.%2C_G.R._-_Singapore_-_Klingalese_family_at_Singapore_-_circa_1900.tif.jpg)

*A South Indian family photographed in Singapore by G. R. Lambert & Co. around 1900. The original caption calls them "Klingalese", a colonial term for South Indians. (Lambert & Co., G.R., KITLV, Leiden University Libraries, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Two Indian men

![A studio portrait of two Indian men in white turbans and white cloths, seated side by side on a carved chair](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/KITLV_-_103787_-_Two_Indian_men_at_Singapore_-_circa_1890.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_-_103787_-_Two_Indian_men_at_Singapore_-_circa_1890.tif.jpg)

*Two Indian men photographed in a Singapore studio around 1890. (Unknown photographer, KITLV, Leiden University Libraries, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A tram in the suburbs

![An open-sided electric tram on a tree-lined road beside a thatched house, with a bullock cart in front of it](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6e/Electric_Tram_Singapore_about_1910.png)

*An electric tram in the suburbs of Singapore around 1910. Electric trams began running in 1905, the year Pillai arrived, and one route ran along Serangoon Road. (Unknown photographer, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### An Indian temple on a tram route

![A postcard of a tall, many-tiered Hindu temple tower over a gateway, with an electric tram and a rickshaw passing in front and tram wires overhead](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Indiase_tempel_te_Singapore%2C_KITLV_25595.tiff/lossy-page1-1280px-Indiase_tempel_te_Singapore%2C_KITLV_25595.tiff.jpg)

*An undated postcard titled "Indian Temple, Singapore", with a tram passing the gateway tower. (Anonymous, KITLV, Leiden University Libraries, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A Hindu temple around 1910

![A Hindu temple with a tall carved gateway tower and a long white wall, seen across a road with a rickshaw](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/KITLV_-_79921_-_Kleingrothe%2C_C.J._-_Medan_-_Klingalese_temple_in_Singapore_-_circa_1910.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_-_79921_-_Kleingrothe%2C_C.J._-_Medan_-_Klingalese_temple_in_Singapore_-_circa_1910.tif.jpg)

*A South Indian temple in Singapore around 1910. (Kleingrothe, C.J., KITLV, Leiden University Libraries, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Nagapattinam on the Coromandel Coast

![An old engraving of a walled port town on a coast, with large sailing ships and small boats in the foreground](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Negapatnam_van_Choromandel.jpg/1280px-Negapatnam_van_Choromandel.jpg)

*Nagapattinam, the port in the Tanjore district through which many Tamil emigrants left for Malaya, in an eighteenth-century Dutch engraving after Johannes Kip. (Unknown engraver after Johannes Kip, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Mayiladuthurai, near his home village

![The courtyard of a large South Indian temple, with a tall gopuram rising behind old weathered buildings](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/24/Mayuranathar8.jpg)

*The Mayuranathar Temple in Mayiladuthurai, the town once called Mayavaram, near Pillai's home village of Koorainadu, in 2015. (விஜயகிருஷ்ணன், CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A silk saree on the loom

![A close view of a deep red silk saree with a gold border being woven on a handloom](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/83/Silk_saree_on_the_making_at_Kanchipuram_%287642281054%29.jpg)

*A silk saree being woven at Kanchipuram in Tamil Nadu, 2012. Saree shops became the best known part of Pillai's business. (Kamal Venkit, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Singapore under the Occupation

![A city street of shophouses with Japanese and Chinese signboards, rickshaws and a car, and Japanese soldiers walking along the road](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fc/Street_in_Shonan.JPG)

*A street in Syonan-to, as Singapore was renamed during the Japanese Occupation, in February 1943. Pillai was in India for the war and lost his property and goods in Singapore. (Unknown photographer, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Across the Causeway

![A car on a road running along a long stone causeway across the water, with telegraph poles beside it](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/KITLV_A1363_-_Weg_van_Djohor_Bahru_naar_Singapore%2C_KITLV_78456.tiff/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_A1363_-_Weg_van_Djohor_Bahru_naar_Singapore%2C_KITLV_78456.tiff.jpg)

*The road from Johor Bahru to Singapore along the Causeway, in an undated photograph. After the war Pillai opened stores across the Causeway in Johor. (Anonymous, KITLV, Leiden University Libraries, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Johor Bahru

![A large white mosque with four minarets on a hill above a lake, with young palm trees along a path in the foreground](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/KITLV_-_79915_-_Kleingrothe%2C_C.J._-_Medan_-_Mosque_at_Johor_-_circa_1910.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_-_79915_-_Kleingrothe%2C_C.J._-_Medan_-_Mosque_at_Johor_-_circa_1910.tif.jpg)

*The Sultan Abu Bakar Mosque in Johor Bahru around 1910. (Kleingrothe, C.J., KITLV, Leiden University Libraries, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### Malacca

![The Dutch Reformed Church and the Stadthuys in Malacca seen across a stone bridge over the river, with a large tree in the middle](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/KITLV_-_101100_-_Kleingrothe%2C_C.J._-_Medan_-_Dutch_Reformed_Church_%28left%29_and_the_Stadt_Huys_%28center_behind_the_tree%29_at_Malacca_%28Melaka%29_in_Malaysia_-_circa_1905.tif/lossy-page1-1280px-thumbnail.tif.jpg)

*The Dutch Reformed Church, left, and the Stadthuys in Malacca around 1905. Pillai's first stores in Malacca were called Dhanalakshmi Stores. (Kleingrothe, C.J., KITLV, Leiden University Libraries, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons)*
