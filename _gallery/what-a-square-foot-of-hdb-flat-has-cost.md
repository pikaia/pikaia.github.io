Four more images from the decades before this chart's 1990 starting point — the kampongs HDB flats replaced, the fire that forced the government's hand, and the building boom that created the market this post tracks.

### The kampong life HDB flats replaced

![A kampong house in Braddell Hill, Singapore, circa 1964](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/81/Kampong_in_Braddell_Hill_Singapore_about_1964.jpg)

*A kampong house in Braddell Hill, Singapore, photographed around 1964 — the kind of housing most Singaporeans lived in before mass HDB resettlement got underway. (Photo: Don Christie / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### The fire that forced the government's hand

![Front page of The Straits Times, 27 May 1961, headlined "Aid for Fire Victims"](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/39/ST27May1961.jpg)

*The Straits Times' front page on 27 May 1961, two days after the Bukit Ho Swee fire left roughly 16,000 people homeless. The government announced it would acquire the razed land to build 12,000 low-cost flats — a turning point for the young Housing and Development Board. (Photo: The Straits Times, courtesy of the National Library Board / Wikimedia Commons, public domain in Singapore)*

### HDB blocks going up in Bukit Merah, 1973-74

![HDB blocks under construction in Bukit Merah, Singapore, 1973-74](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Singapore-Public_Housing-1973-74-WUS08215.jpg/960px-Singapore-Public_Housing-1973-74-WUS08215.jpg)

*Public housing blocks under construction in Bukit Merah, from a color slide shot in 1973-74, when HDB building was running at its fastest pace. (Photo: Rainer Halama / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### A skyline being remade by public housing

![Aerial view of Singapore's skyline in 1973-74, showing rows of HDB slab blocks among the city's towers](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/Singapore-Public_Housing-1973-74-WUS08216.jpg/960px-Singapore-Public_Housing-1973-74-WUS08216.jpg)

*An aerial view of Singapore in 1973-74. The rows of near-identical white slab blocks in the middle distance are HDB estates — part of a building boom that had already reshaped the skyline well before this post's price chart even begins, in 1990. (Photo: Rainer Halama / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*
