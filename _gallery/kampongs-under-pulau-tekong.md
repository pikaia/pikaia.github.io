Genuine photographs of Tekong's own kampongs are almost nonexistent in public archives — the island was cleared before its villages were widely photographed — so these five images instead capture the wider world of Malay kampong life in Singapore, from an offshore island's own fishing village to mainland scenes of the same kind and era.

### Pulau Brani, an island kampong like Tekong's own

![Malay village on Pulau Brani, Singapore, circa 1900](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/06/Malay_-_village_Pulo_Brani_Singapore_%28NYPL_Hades-2359734-4044499%29.jpg)

*A Malay fishing village on Pulau Brani, one of Singapore's own offshore islands, photographed around 1900 — the same kind of stilt-and-attap settlement that once stood on Pulau Tekong before both were cleared. (Photo: New York Public Library, Pacific Pursuits postcard collection / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### A Malay kampong in Singapore, 1907

![Malay village, Singapore, 1907](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/da/Malay_village%2C_Singapore_%28NYPL_Hades-2359721-4044486%29.jpg)

*A Malay kampong in Singapore, photographed in 1907 — the thatched, attap-roofed houses typical of the villages that once covered Pulau Tekong and the wider Johor Strait coastline. (Photo: New York Public Library, Pacific Pursuits postcard collection / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### Kampong Baru, circa 1890

![Kampong Baru at Singapore, circa 1890](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/KITLV_-_105811_-_Lambert_%26_Co.%2C_G.R._-_Singapore_-_Kampong_Baru_at_Singapore_-_circa_1890.tif/lossy-page1-960px-KITLV_-_105811_-_Lambert_%26_Co.%2C_G.R._-_Singapore_-_Kampong_Baru_at_Singapore_-_circa_1890.tif.jpg)

*Kampong Baru at Singapore, photographed by G.R. Lambert & Co. around 1890 — one of the many named kampongs that dotted colonial-era Singapore, the same kind and scale as those later founded on Tekong by migrants fleeing Pahang's civil war. (Photo: G.R. Lambert & Co., KITLV collection / Leiden University Library / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### Kampong Buggis, the Buginese quarter, circa 1900

![Kampong Buggis, the Buginese district of Singapore, circa 1900](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/KITLV_-_105810_-_Lambert_%26_Co.%2C_G.R._-_Singapore_-_Kampong_Buggis%2C_the_Buginese_district_of_Singapore_-_circa_1900.tif/lossy-page1-960px-KITLV_-_105810_-_Lambert_%26_Co.%2C_G.R._-_Singapore_-_Kampong_Buggis%2C_the_Buginese_district_of_Singapore_-_circa_1900.tif.jpg)

*Kampong Buggis, the Buginese quarter of Singapore, photographed by G.R. Lambert & Co. around 1900. Bugis migrants were among the many communities who settled Singapore's kampongs, on the mainland and its offshore islands alike. (Photo: G.R. Lambert & Co., KITLV collection / Leiden University Library / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### A kampong at Braddell Hill, about 1964

![A kampong at Braddell Hill, Singapore, about 1964](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/81/Kampong_in_Braddell_Hill_Singapore_about_1964.jpg)

*A kampong at Braddell Hill, Singapore, photographed around 1964 — closer in time to the Tekong villages of living memory, not long before redevelopment began clearing kampongs like it across Singapore. (Photo: Don Christie / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*
