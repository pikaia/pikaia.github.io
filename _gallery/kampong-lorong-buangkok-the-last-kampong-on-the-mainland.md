The lanes, houses and prayer house of Singapore's last mainland kampong — photographed by visitors over the past two decades, in a village that is not a museum but twenty-five families' home.

### The way in

![The signboard at the entrance to Kampong Lorong Buangkok](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5f/Lorong_Buangkok_Singapore_signage.JPG)

*The brown signboard, in English and Chinese, that marks the kampong's inconspicuous entrance off Gerald Drive. (Zabaguio, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2014)*

### A typical kampong house

![A typical kampong house in Lorong Buangkok, photographed in 2008](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/07/A_Typical_Kampong_House.JPG)

*Timber walls, a zinc roof, and a garden instead of a fence — the standard kampong house, photographed in 2008, the year the Master Plan first drew a road through the village. (Grps, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons)*

### A village house

![A wooden village house among trees in Kampong Lorong Buangkok](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/92/Lorong_Buangkok_house.JPG)

*One of the twenty-five homes, each built by its family on a rented plot — the tenants own their houses; what they rent is the ground. (Zarina Baguio, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2014)*

### An entrance door

![The entrance door of a house in Kampong Lorong Buangkok](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/11/An_entrance_door_in_Kampong_Lorong_Buangkok.jpg)

*A house door in 2026. The doors that visitors in the 2000s found standing open are likelier to be shut today — the price of being famous. (M. Adrian Jurhami, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2026)*

### The surau's washing area

![The ablution area of Surau Al-Firdaus](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c8/Surau_Al_Firdaus_at_Kampong_Lorong_Buangkok_3.jpg)

*The ablution area of Surau Al-Firdaus. The prayer house, first built in 1953, serves Muslims from the surrounding estates as well as the kampong's own families. (Muhammad Adrian, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2025)*

### Power to a wooden house

![An electricity meter fixed to a wooden kampong house](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/22/Photo_Journal_%28Set%29_-_A_power_supply_meter_to_a_wooden_kampung_house_%289682076337%29.jpg)

*An electricity meter on a wooden wall. Running water and electricity only reached the kampong in 1962, six years after the first families built here. (Erwin Soo, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2013)*

### The earthenware stove

![A traditional earthenware stove in the kampong](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/32/Photo_Journal_%28set%29_-_An_earthernware_stove_%289719616498%29.jpg)

*An earthenware stove — the older kitchen, kept. Much of the kampong's daily life still runs at this pace. (Erwin Soo, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2013)*

### Chickens at home

![Chickens roaming in Kampong Lorong Buangkok](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/91/Photo_Journal_%28Set%29_-_Shoo%21_%289695528234%29.jpg)

*Chickens roam the lanes freely, with the kampong's cats and dogs — the detail every account of the village mentions, because no HDB estate sounds like this. (Erwin Soo, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2013)*

### Young explorers

![Children exploring the kampong on a visit](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3c/Photo_Journal_%28Set%29_-_Young_explorers_%289719102704%29.jpg)

*Young visitors exploring, 2013. School groups and guided tours are now the sanctioned way in — the village receives its guests on its own terms. (Erwin Soo, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2013)*

### Taken back by green

![An overgrown structure in the kampong, vegetation reclaiming it](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/85/Photo_Journal_%28Set%29_-_Overgrown_%289685319060%29_%282%29.jpg)

*When a house falls empty here, the green takes it back quickly. A vacancy is offered only to someone connected to the village — the kampong has had no newcomers since the 1990s. (Erwin Soo, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; photographed 2013)*
