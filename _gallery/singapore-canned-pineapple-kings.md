A few more images of the men who built fortunes on canned pineapple and the institutions that outlasted the fruit — the industry itself left almost no photographic record behind.

### Tan Kah Kee

![Portrait of Tan Kah Kee, around the 1930s to 1940s](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7b/%E9%99%88%E5%98%89%E5%BA%9A2.jpg)

*Tan Kah Kee around the 1930s–1940s, at the height of his standing as a businessman and educational patron. (National Archives of Singapore / Tan Kah Kee Memorial Museum, via Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### The Chinese High School, founded by Tan Kah Kee in 1919

![The clock tower building of The Chinese High School, now Hwa Chong Institution, Singapore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b2/%E6%96%B0%E5%8A%A0%E5%9D%A1%E5%8D%97%E6%B4%8B%E5%8D%8E%E4%BE%A8%E4%B8%AD%E5%AD%A6%E9%92%9F%E6%A5%BC.JPG)

*The clock tower block of The Chinese High School, the school Tan Kah Kee founded in 1919 and Lim Nee Soon helped establish; it is now part of Hwa Chong Institution. Photographed in 2010. (Gisling / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0)*

### The same school in 1952

![A classroom building at The Chinese High School photographed in 1952](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/95/Classroom_building_in_the_50s.JPG)

*A classroom building at The Chinese High School, photographed in 1952 — around the time the last of the pineapple canneries were leaving Singapore for Johor. (Gisling / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0)*

### Nee Soon Road, 1980s

![Nee Soon Road, Singapore, photographed in the 1980s](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6b/Nee_Soon_Road.jpg)

*Nee Soon Road in the 1980s, around the time the wider district was renamed Yishun. The road kept the older spelling. (daves_archive / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0)*
