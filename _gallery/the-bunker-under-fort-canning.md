Fourteen more images from the story that played out above and around the Battlebox — the hill across six centuries of legend and empire, the commanders on both sides of the table, the day the decision made underground became public, and what came after.

### Long before the war: the legend that named the hill

![Sang Nila Utama Garden, Fort Canning Hill](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/31/Sang_nila_utama_garden.jpg)

*A garden on Fort Canning Hill commemorating Sang Nila Utama, the legendary Sumatran prince said to have founded Singapore here in the 14th century — the hill was known as Bukit Larangan, "Forbidden Hill," long before it became a British command post. (Photo: ScribblingGeek / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### The earliest known drawing of Singapore

![Singapore from the Sea, June 1823, sketched by Lt. Philip Jackson](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8a/Singapore_from_the_Sea_June_1823_-_Lt._Phillip_Jackson.jpg)

*The earliest known drawing of the settlement, sketched from the sea on 5 June 1823 — Raffles's residence is visible on the hill, beside a flagpole, less than four years after his landing. (Photo: Philip Jackson / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### The hill in 1825, when it was still a signal station

![Part of Singapore Island, British Library India Office Records, 1825, detail](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9f/Part_of_Singapore_Island_%28British_Library_India_Office_Records%2C_1825%2C_detail%29.jpg)

*A British survey map of Singapore from 1825, just six years after Raffles's landing, marking the hill's "Flag Staff" — the signal station that gave Fort Canning Hill its earlier name, Bukit Larangan. (British Library India Office Records, via Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### The flagstaff that watched over the harbour

![Fort Canning from the Singapore River, 1860-1900](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/97/Fort_Canning_from_the_Singapore_River_-_1860%E2%80%931900.jpg)

*Fort Canning Hill viewed from the Singapore River sometime between 1860 and 1900, its signal mast still visible above the trees. (The National Archives, UK / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### Fort Canning Hill before the war

![Fort Canning Hill, Singapore, circa 1902](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/91/Fort_Canning_Hill-1902.jpg)

*Fort Canning Hill, photographed around 1902 — decades before the Malaya Command headquarters and its underground bunker were built into the hillside. (Photo: Aldwin Teo / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA)*

### What's left of the hill's earlier residents

![The Old Christian Cemetery at Fort Canning Park](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/80/20250628_oldchristiancemetery.jpg)

*Headstones from the 1820s to 1860s lining the wall of the Old Christian Cemetery, relocated here when the hill was cleared for military use — long before the Battlebox was ever dug into it. (Photo: Wombatjpw / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### General Percival arrives in Singapore

![Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival arriving in Singapore, 1941](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/39/Lieutenant_General_Arthur_Percival.jpg)

*Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival on his arrival in Singapore as General Officer Commanding Malaya, 13 August 1941 — six months before he would order the surrender from the bunker beneath Fort Canning Hill. (Photo: Imperial War Museums / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### The garrison's positions, days before the end

![Singapore Island, Dispositions of the Garrison, February 1942](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e8/Dispositions_of_the_Garrison_February-1942.png)

*An official British military map showing troop positions across Singapore Island in February 1942 — the kind of map that would have hung on the Battlebox's walls as the situation collapsed. (Photo: Her Majesty's Stationery Office / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### Percival meets the press, weeks before the end

![Lieutenant General Percival with war correspondents in Singapore, late January 1942](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ad/Percival_with_war_correspondents.jpg)

*Lieutenant-General Percival (second from right) with war correspondents in Singapore, late January 1942 — shortly before the capitulation. (Photo: Lt. Palmer / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### General Yamashita, on the other side of the table

![General Tomoyuki Yamashita, 1942](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/21/Yamashita_e_Suzuki.jpg)

*General Tomoyuki Yamashita, commander of the Japanese 25th Army, in 1942. It was Yamashita who demanded Percival's unconditional surrender at the Ford Factory on Bukit Timah. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### The general above Yamashita

![General Terauchi Hisaichi in Singapore, 1942](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2c/Terauchi_Hisaichi_in_Singapore_1942.png)

*General Count Terauchi Hisaichi, commander of the Japanese Southern Expeditionary Army Group and Yamashita's superior, in Singapore, 1942. (Photo: Japanese Press / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### British troops surrender, 15 February 1942

![British troops surrender in Singapore, 15 February 1942](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fb/British_troops_surrender_in_Singapore.png)

*British troops surrender to Japanese forces in Singapore, 15 February 1942 — the day the decision reached inside the Battlebox became public. (Photo: Australian War Memorial / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### Walking out under a flag of truce

![Lieutenant-General Percival's party marches under a flag of truce, Singapore, 15 February 1942](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/74/Singaporesurrender.jpg)

*Lieutenant-General Percival's party marches under a flag of truce to negotiate the surrender of Singapore, 15 February 1942 — the walk that followed the decision made in the bunker beneath Fort Canning Hill. (Photo: UK Government / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### What came after

![Liberated British prisoners of war in the Far East, 1945](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d9/Liberated_British_Prisoners_of_War_in_the_Far_East%2C_1945%2C_HU_69968.jpg)

*Former British prisoners of war, recently liberated from captivity in Sumatra, in Singapore in 1945 — one holding a newspaper announcing the atomic bomb. (Photo: Oliver Zina, Imperial War Museums / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*
