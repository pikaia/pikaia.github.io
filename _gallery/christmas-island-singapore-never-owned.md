Five more images tracing the mechanics behind the myth — the phosphate railway that made the island worth administering, the 1942 occupation that exposed how exposed it was, and the paper trail from both sides of the 1958 handover.

### The railway that made the island worth having

![Steam locomotive built for the Christmas Island Phosphate Company's railway, 1931](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b4/0-8-0_tender_locomotive_for_the_Christmas_Island_Phosphate_Co.%27s_Railway_by_Peckett_%26_Sons_Ltd._of_Bristol%2C_No._1824_of_March_1931.jpg)

*A 0-8-0 tender locomotive built by Peckett & Sons of Bristol for the Christmas Island Phosphate Co.'s railway, photographed on completion in March 1931. Phosphate royalties like the ones this line hauled to port were the actual income at stake in 1958 — not land Singapore never held title to. (Photo: The Locomotive Magazine and Railway Carriage & Wagon Review, 1931 / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### The 1942 occupation that exposed the island's vulnerability

![Japanese naval troops posing with a captured British coastal gun on Christmas Island, April 1942](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/dd/Japanese_Marines_in_Christmas_Island_1942.jpg)

*Imperial Japanese special naval landing force troops pose with a captured British coastal gun after taking Christmas Island without resistance in April 1942, following a mutiny among the island's Indian garrison. The episode sharpened Australia's postwar interest in controlling the phosphate supply directly. (Photo: Japanese wartime press, 1942 / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### The minister who said Singapore never owned it

![Portrait of Abdul Hamid Jumat](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/88/Abdul_Hamid_bin_Haji_Jumat.jpg)

*Abdul Hamid Jumat, Singapore's Acting Chief Minister in 1958, photographed in 1965 while serving as Malaysia's ambassador to West Germany. It was Jumat who told the Legislative Assembly plainly that Christmas Island "was asked to administer it and nothing more." (Photo: Renate Patzek / Bundesarchiv, Bild 145 Bild-F021432-0010 / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0 DE)*

### The paperwork of a sovereignty change

![Christmas Island's first postage stamp, 1958, an Australian stamp overprinted "Christmas Island"](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/67/Stamp_Christmas_Island_1958_2c.jpg)

*Christmas Island's first postage stamp, issued in 1958 — an Australian definitive overprinted "CHRISTMAS ISLAND," marking the island's new status as Australian territory the same year Singapore's administrative link to it ended. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### The self-government Singapore actually won, a year later

![Front page of The Sunday Times, Singapore, 31 May 1959, reporting the PAP's landslide election victory](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/95/ST31May1959.jpg)

*The Sunday Times' front page on 31 May 1959, reporting the PAP's landslide election victory under Lee Kuan Yew (with Lim Yew Hock also pictured) — the full internal self-government Singapore secured a year after it lost its administrative claim on Christmas Island, with no say in the matter. (Photo: The Straits Times, 1959 / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*
