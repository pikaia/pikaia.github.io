Four more views of Syonan Jinja — how it appeared in wartime print and on a postage stamp, and what's left of the Divine Bridge in MacRitchie's forest today.

### The shrine in wartime print

![Syonan Shrine as pictured in Djawa Baroe magazine, June 1943](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d7/Syonan_Shrine_in_Singapore%2C_Djawa_Baroe%2C_Vol._1%2C_Iss._11_%281943-06-01%29%2C_p31.jpg)

*Syonan Jinja pictured in* Djawa Baroe*, a Japanese-run magazine circulated across occupied Southeast Asia, June 1943. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### On a wartime postage stamp

![A Japanese-occupation postage stamp featuring Syonan Jinja](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8f/%E6%98%AD%E5%8D%97%E7%A5%9E%E7%A4%BE%E9%82%AE%E7%A5%A8%EF%BC%88%E6%97%A5%E6%B2%BB%E9%A9%AC%E6%9D%A5%EF%BC%89.jpg)

### The Divine Bridge's ruins, another angle

![Remains of the Divine Bridge leading to Syonan Jinja, MacRitchie Reservoir](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/33/Remains_of_Divine_Bridge_leading_to_Synonan_Jinja_at_MacRitchie_Reservoir%2C_Singapore%2C_2_-_2022-07-02.jpg)
