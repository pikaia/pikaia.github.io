A few more views of the Causeway and its surroundings — the crossing in the mid-20th century, the water pipelines it carries, the checkpoints at each end, the newer Second Link, the Johor Bahru malls a Singaporean day trip usually ends up in, and the rail station being built for the shuttle that is meant to relieve it.

### The Causeway around 1950

![A sepia photograph of a single car crossing the Causeway on the two-lane road, with the rubble stone embankment on the right and a railway line behind a fence on the left](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/KITLV_A1363_-_Weg_van_Djohor_Bahru_naar_Singapore%2C_KITLV_78456.tiff/lossy-page1-1280px-KITLV_A1363_-_Weg_van_Djohor_Bahru_naar_Singapore%2C_KITLV_78456.tiff.jpg)

*The Causeway as a quiet road crossing around 1950, looking towards Johor Bahru. The rubble-stone embankment is on the right; the railway line runs behind the fence on the left. (Photograph from the travel album of Frans de Waard; Leiden University Library, KITLV collection, [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).)*

### The water pipelines, 1973

![A colour photograph taken from a vehicle crossing the Causeway in the 1970s, showing large water pipes on trestles running alongside the road and railway, with a loaded lorry ahead](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/Singapore-Johore-Causeway-1973-74-WUS08290.jpg/1280px-Singapore-Johore-Causeway-1973-74-WUS08290.jpg)

*Crossing the Causeway by car in 1973 or 1974. The pipelines carrying Johor water to Singapore run along the left, beside the single railway track; a Johor-registered lorry shares the road. (Photo: Rainer Halama / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).)*

### The Causeway by day

![A daytime view of the Johor–Singapore Causeway as a low grey embankment across the Johor Strait, seen from the Singapore side](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/Johor_Causeway.jpg/1280px-Johor_Causeway.jpg)

*The Causeway seen from Woodlands Waterfront on the Singapore side, with Johor Bahru beyond. It is a solid bank of rock carrying road, rail and water pipes — not a bridge. (Photo: Johan Jönsson / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).)*

### The Woodlands train checkpoint

![The Woodlands Train Checkpoint building in Singapore, where rail passengers are cleared](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/Woodlands_Train_Checkpoint.jpg/1280px-Woodlands_Train_Checkpoint.jpg)

*The Woodlands Train Checkpoint, where passengers on the shuttle train from Johor Bahru clear Singapore immigration. The Causeway completed Singapore's rail link to the peninsula in 1923. (Photo: Amy00 / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/).)*

### The Sultan Iskandar checkpoint, Johor Bahru

![The Sultan Iskandar Customs, Immigration and Quarantine complex on the Malaysian side of the Causeway](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/SultanIskandarCIQ.JPG/1280px-SultanIskandarCIQ.JPG)

*The Sultan Iskandar CIQ complex at Bukit Chagar, the Malaysian end of the Causeway. When it opened in 2008, crossing the Causeway on foot was prohibited on the Malaysian side. (Photo: Jani Patokallio / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/).)*

### The "chop"

![A Singapore immigration entry stamp in a Malaysian passport, with a border of small letter W markings indicating Woodlands Checkpoint](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Singapore_Woodlands_Checkpoint_entry_stamp_in_a_passport_-_20070720.jpg/1280px-Singapore_Woodlands_Checkpoint_entry_stamp_in_a_passport_-_20070720.jpg)

*A Singapore entry stamp in a Malaysian passport, Woodlands Checkpoint, 2007 — the border of small "W"s marks it as Woodlands. The stamp is mostly gone now: clearance at the land checkpoints runs through automated gates that read a passport, or a QR code in the vehicle lanes, and take a photo, without an officer or an ink stamp. (Photo: Timothyngim / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/).)*

### Woodlands Checkpoint today

![The Woodlands Checkpoint building in December 2023, with buses lined up and a crowd of pedestrians walking towards the entrance](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Woodlands_Checkpoint_December_2023.jpg/1280px-Woodlands_Checkpoint_December_2023.jpg)

*Woodlands Checkpoint, December 2023 — the Singapore end of the Causeway. It is being rebuilt over the coming decade to handle far more travellers. (Photo: ZKang123 / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).)*

### Where the land checkpoints are headed

![A row of automated immigration gates at Changi Airport Terminal 4](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Terminal_4_%28Changi%29_Immigration_automated_gate_entrance.jpg/1280px-Terminal_4_%28Changi%29_Immigration_automated_gate_entrance.jpg)

*Automated immigration gates at Changi Airport Terminal 4, where clearance is now on iris and face alone, with no passport. The land checkpoints already use automated gates — a passport or QR-code scan and a photo, no officer — and are being upgraded towards the airport's fully passport-free version: facial recognition for motorcyclists from 2026, automated in-vehicle clearance from 2027. (Photo: Ominae / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).)*

### The Second Link

![The Malaysia–Singapore Second Link bridge, seen from a vehicle leaving the Tuas checkpoint](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/09/Malaysia-Singapore_Second_Link_bridge.jpg/1280px-Malaysia-Singapore_Second_Link_bridge.jpg)

*The Second Link between Tuas and Gelang Patah, opened in 1998 as a second road crossing. It relieved some traffic without ever really easing the Causeway. (Photo: Nigos / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).)*

### Where the day trip ends up

![An AEON shopping mall in Bukit Indah, Johor Bahru, with a rainbow overhead](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Aeon_Mall_and_a_rainbow%2C_Bukit_Indah%2C_Johor_Bahru%2C_Johor%2C_Malaysia.jpg/1280px-Aeon_Mall_and_a_rainbow%2C_Bukit_Indah%2C_Johor_Bahru%2C_Johor%2C_Malaysia.jpg)

*An AEON mall in Bukit Indah, Johor Bahru — hypermarket, food court, clinics and services, and a car park that fills with Singapore plates on a weekend. (Photo: Sharon Hahn Darlin / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/).)*

### The shuttle being built

![The Bukit Chagar RTS Link station under construction in Johor Bahru, June 2026, a large domed structure under blue scaffolding beside a new terminal building and elevated viaducts](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Bukit_Chagar_RTS_station_construction_site_20260613_175634.jpg/1280px-Bukit_Chagar_RTS_station_construction_site_20260613_175634.jpg)

*The Bukit Chagar station for the Johor Bahru–Singapore RTS Link, under construction in June 2026. The cross-border rail shuttle to Woodlands North is due to open around the start of 2027. (Photo: ZKang123 / Wikimedia Commons, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).)*
