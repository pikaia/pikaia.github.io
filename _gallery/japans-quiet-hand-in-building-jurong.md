Five more images tracing the road from occupation to industrial partner — the 1945 surrender that started the clock, the harbour trade Jurong was meant to move Singapore beyond, and the swampy west-side land and early estate housing that became the country's first industrial estate.

### The surrender that started the clock

![Lord Mountbatten addresses the crowd at the Japanese surrender ceremony, Singapore, 12 September 1945](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a9/Signing_of_the_Japanese_Surrender_at_Singapore%2C_1945_CF720.jpg)

*Admiral Lord Louis Mountbatten addresses the crowd from the steps of the Municipal Building during the formal Japanese surrender ceremony in Singapore, 12 September 1945 — the starting point for the "barely eighteen years" this post opens with. Photographed by Flight Officer Ashley, Royal Air Force official photographer. (Photo: Imperial War Museum collection / Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

### The trade Jurong was built to move beyond

![Singapore's harbour roadstead crowded with anchored ships, 1963](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4e/Singapore_roadstead_1963_01.jpg)

*Singapore's roadstead anchorage in 1963, the year Jurong Shipyard was incorporated — the entrepot trade that was still the backbone of the economy while the EDB went looking for manufacturers instead. (Photo: Don Christie / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### The land west of the city, before the factories

![A Malay village near Tengah, Singapore, October 1964](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/ca/Malay_village_near_Tengah_Singapore_October_1964.jpg)

*A kampong near Tengah, just north of Jurong, photographed in October 1964 — the kind of rural landscape the reclaimed mudflats of the new industrial estate were carved out of. (Photo: Don Christie / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### The road into the new estate

![Winding road leading to Jurong staff quarters, 1967](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/06/1967_-_Winding_Road_to_Jurong_Staff_Quarters.jpg)

*The road into Jurong's Telecoms staff quarters, photographed in 1967 — one of the residential pockets built alongside the shipyards and factories to house the estate's growing workforce. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons user Woonyt, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### Housing for the workforce the estate needed

![Staff quarters at Jurong, Singapore, 1967, home to around 200 residents](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/47/Jurong_Staff_Quarters.jpg)

*Staff quarters in Jurong housing around 200 residents in 1967 — the domestic side of an industrial estate that, within a few years, would include a Japanese-run shipyard and tyre plant. (Photo: Wikimedia Commons user Woonyt, CC BY-SA 4.0)*
