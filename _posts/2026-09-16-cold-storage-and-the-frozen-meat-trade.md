On 8 June 1903, a company called Cold Storage was registered in Singapore to solve one very specific problem: there was no reliable way, in the tropics, to keep a piece of meat cold long enough to eat it safely. Everything the company became afterwards — the supermarkets, the ice cream, the dairy farm, even a shopping mall on Orchard Road — grew out of that one narrow need.

## A wharf shed with a very specific purpose

Cold Storage's first cargo, imported chilled meat, poultry and dairy products from Queensland, Australia, went into a 400-tonne refrigeration facility on a wharf at Keppel Harbour. The company opened its first retail shop on 24 March 1905, at the corner of Cuppage Road and Orchard Road. It did not do well at first: domestic servants, who did much of the everyday household shopping for European families, largely avoided it, and the company's early attempts at importing fresh fruit and vegetables mostly spoiled on the sea journey before they ever reached a shelf. Business only recovered from 1909, under new management.

## From ice to institution

The company kept building on the same basic idea — reliable, controlled cold — well beyond meat. In 1923 it opened a small ice cream factory at Borneo Wharf under the brand name Paradise. In 1937 its managing director, an Australian named Fred Heron, founded what the company called the world's first tropical dairy farm, at Bukit Timah: some 60 acres and, at its peak, around 800 cows, producing pasteurised milk for a market that had never had fresh local milk before. Paradise ice cream was relaunched that same year under a new name, Magnolia — a brand that outlived Cold Storage's own ownership of it and is still a familiar name in Singapore supermarkets today. In 1959, Cold Storage's Orchard Road outlet became Singapore's first self-service supermarket, a format almost every grocery shop on the island now uses without a second thought.

## A mall built by a grocer

By the early 1980s, Cold Storage was successful enough to become a property developer in its own right. In November 1983 the Cold Storage Group opened Centrepoint, the shopping mall that still stands on Orchard Road today, and moved its own flagship supermarket into it. For decades afterward, "the Cold Storage at Centrepoint" was simply where a certain kind of Singapore household did its grocery shopping.

My own family was a typical Chinese household, and Christmas was a newer, lighter occasion for us growing up — we celebrated, but with different food. It was only after I married my wife, whose family kept a bigger, more established Eurasian-style Christmas built around particular dishes, that ham became part of our own celebrations. Every year before Christmas we'd go to the Cold Storage on Orchard Road, behind Centrepoint, to buy a raw ham. The first time we brought home one glazed in pineapple and honey, it was such a hit that it became a fixture — and it's the specific memory that ties our family, in a small way, to a company that started out solving a completely different problem eighty years earlier.

## Sold, and sold again

Cold Storage's own ownership changed more than once. In 1992, Dairy Farm International — part of the Jardine Matheson group — bought Cold Storage Holdings' retail operations for S$130 million. That arrangement lasted over three decades, until March 2025, when DFI Retail Group sold its entire Singapore food business — 48 Cold Storage, CS Fresh and Jason's Deli stores, 41 Giant stores, and two distribution centres — to Macrovalue, a Malaysian retail group, for S$125 million. Macrovalue's new owners promised lower prices and more choices; the deal was expected to close in the second half of 2025.

Cold Storage is, if anything, more visible today than it has ever been — a premium-priced alternative sitting alongside NTUC FairPrice and Sheng Siong on almost every shopping street, in neighbourhoods that have nothing to do with its old European clientele. On a visit back to Singapore, I stayed in Joo Chiat and was surprised to find a Cold Storage branch right there, a short walk from the old Peranakan shophouses — not the kind of address I'd have expected for a brand that built its name on chilled Australian mutton for colonial households. It's pricier than the other chains, and it's still there anyway.

**Why it matters today:** most people walking into a Cold Storage today are just doing their weekly shopping, in a supermarket format the company itself introduced to Singapore in 1959. Almost nobody buying a ham there at Christmas is thinking about a 400-tonne refrigeration shed on a wharf in 1903, or a dairy farm with 800 cows in Bukit Timah, or the fact that the mall around them was built by the grocer, not the other way round.
