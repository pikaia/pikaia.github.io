NOTE: The National Museum of Singapore does NOT take these by email. Use the
NMS Image Use Request Form: https://form.gov.sg/5ee71aa4b775c800116e5eae
Processing ~14 working days; NHB may attach licence fees or decline without
reason. The text below is what to paste into the form's free-text fields.

Also note: a low-resolution public-domain copy exists on Wikimedia Commons —
"Four drawings of plants from Malacca (William Farquhar Collection, 1819–1823)"
— but it is a 500 x 522 px composite of four plants (gambier, black pepper,
durian, wild nutmeg), too small and too crowded to use as a standalone gambier
image. This request is for the single-plant gambier drawing as reproduced in
BiblioAsia (see below).

--- FORM CONTENT ---

Image requested:
"Gambier" (Nauclea gambir / Uncaria gambir; "Pokok Gambir"), a watercolour
from the William Farquhar Collection of Natural History Drawings, National
Museum of Singapore (collection commissioned by William Farquhar, Malay
Peninsula, 1803–1818). As reproduced in the BiblioAsia article "When Tigers
Used to Roam" (National Library Board, Vol. 13 Issue 4, Jan–Mar 2018),
https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/all-sections/vol-13-issue-4-jan-mar-2018-when-tigers-used-to-roam/
— credited there "Courtesy of National Museum of Singapore, National Heritage
Board." If a catalogue/accession number is needed, please advise which record
this corresponds to.

Requestor / project:
Chris Lee, chriskslee@gmail.com. Lesser Known Singapore
(https://pikaia.github.io, https://www.youtube.com/@LesserKnownSingapore), a
small non-commercial blog on overlooked Singapore history.

Purpose of use:
A single inline illustration in a blog post about the history of wild tigers
in Singapore. The post discusses the 19th-century gambier and pepper
plantations whose spread into the forest drove the tiger attacks of that
period; the drawing would illustrate the gambier plant itself. Non-commercial,
web only, with full credit exactly as NHB specifies and a link back.

Resolution:
Web resolution is sufficient; a 300 dpi file is not required.

Questions:
Please advise the permitted use, the exact credit line, any conditions, and
any licence fee for this non-commercial use.
