Title:
When America Crashed, Singapore Sank: The Great Depression and the Contagion That Still Reaches Us

Description:
In 1929 a pound of Malayan rubber sold for an average of 34 cents. By June 1932 it sold for 4.95 cents, the lowest price ever recorded. Singapore did not grow rubber, but it lived by processing, financing and shipping the rubber and tin of the Malay Peninsula, and much of both went to the United States, where the car industry had collapsed after the Wall Street crash of 1929. Singapore's later recessions have arrived by other routes as well, and setting them side by side shows a pattern: a crash abroad does the most damage when it cuts demand for the goods Singapore depends on most. That pattern is worth knowing today, when a large part of the island's growth comes from making the chips and equipment behind artificial intelligence.
