Title:
Letters with Money: Singapore's Remittance Houses and the Savings Sent Home to China

Description:
For more than a century, a Chinese labourer in Singapore who wanted to send part of his wages to his family in China did not go to a bank. He went to a remittance house, often a tea merchant, a medicine hall or a grocer in the back streets near the river, which took his money and his letter, sent them to his home village in Fujian or Guangdong, and weeks later brought him back a reply. The letter that travelled with the money was called a qiaopi, and Singapore was the hub of the trade for the whole of Southeast Asia, with more remittance houses at one point than the rest of the region combined. When the colonial government tried to take the business over in 1876, it set off one of the town's most serious riots.
