Title:
Who Pays for the News? Singapore Has Been Asking Since 1824

Description:
Singapore's first newspaper was printed on rough Chinese-made paper, folded into four small pages and sold for 18 Spanish dollars a year. It got through its first five years on a government subsidy of 60 Spanish dollars a month, and once that subsidy was withdrawn and competitors arrived, it did not last another decade. Nearly two centuries later, the question that decided the Singapore Chronicle's fate, who pays for local news in a small market, is being answered again, and public money is once more part of the answer.
