Title:
Horsburgh Lighthouse: Building the Light on Pedra Branca

Description:
On 24 May 1850, Queen Victoria's birthday, a steam frigate towed two ships out to a bare granite rock 37 miles east of Singapore. On board were the Governor of the Straits Settlements, an admiral, the foreign consuls and the Freemasons of Lodge Zetland in the East, who had come to lay a foundation stone with Masonic honours. The rock was Pedra Branca, and the stone was the first of a lighthouse that would carry the name of the hydrographer James Horsburgh. Its architect, the government surveyor John Turnbull Thomson, wrote a long account of how it was built, and more than 150 years later the building and running of that lighthouse were part of the evidence when the International Court of Justice decided who owns the rock.
